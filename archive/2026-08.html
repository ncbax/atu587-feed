The President's Report: Seattle Transit Measure Renewal

By Greg Woodfill · Pages 1, 24 · Open in the PDF

On July 21st, the Seattle City Council voted unanimously to send a new STM to Seattle voters this November. Seattle voters will decide whether to increase the city's current transit sales tax from 0.15% to 0.30% through 2037.

The proposal would direct 86% of this revenue towards funding transit service ($94.5 million for Bus Service; $23.4 million for Seattle Streetcar operations). If approved by Seattle voters, the 2026 Seattle Transit Measure Renewal would replace the expiring 2020 Seattle Transit Measure. The expiring STM generates over $50 million annually until April 2027 to fund transit investments.

The 2026 STM Renewal Proposal seeks to fund:

  • 280,000 bus trips in Seattle each year, 100,000 more trips than today
  • Better bus service 24/7. That means more trips on Saturdays and Sundays, and more trips midday and overnight outside traditional commuter peaks
  • Continued focus on service for populations who depend on transit most
  • Free ORCA cards for 22,000 people to lower the barrier to riding transit for low-income people, older adults, and families with young children
  • The two Seattle Streetcar lines, providing access to local destinations, jobs, schools, and the regional light rail network
  • Targeted construction projects where buses get delayed, decreasing travel times and increasing reliability
  • Expedite permits and construction oversight to build light rail quickly and safely, while improving access to future stations including Graham Street and along Sound Transit's West Seattle and Ballard Link Extension projects.

This Seattle Transit Measure represents $1.2 billion invested in transit service over 10 years, which represents ATU 587 jobs. In 2020, ATU 587 contributed $50,000 to the Yes campaign for the current STM, and the International contributed an additional $50,000. ATU's combined $100,000 was the highest contribution, as it should be since this represents 587 jobs. It's important to show all our voice and power. Our Union's contributions helped fuel an 80% yes vote!

Given how much is at stake, the doubling of the tax, the 10-year length, and the increased costs of advertising and mailers since 2020. We will be asking the membership to contribute $75,000 for this Yes on Prop 1 campaign and request that ATU International consider matching our donation.

We fully understand many of our members' disagreements with sales tax increases, and Seattle politics in general. We should not take a chance on losing this funding, which could result in fewer living wage ATU 587 jobs, versus more 587 jobs if it passes. Preliminary polling shows a little less than 60% Seattle Voter approval for this proposed sales tax increase to increase transit service. There are real risks that anti-tax proponents with deep pockets could decide to fund a serious no campaign. It's up to the membership whether we make this investment. Hopefully, by the time you read this, the membership will have approved this contribution.

ATU 587 will be a critical part of a coalition endorsing and fighting to pass this Seattle Transit Measure, made up of the Transit Choices Coalition, the Transit Riders Union, and other pro-transit groups. Our Union will be listed on the Yes on Prop 1 campaign and listed in the voters' pamphlet as a joint author of the statement of support. Our Union will explore potential volunteer opportunities for our members who want to help drive up the yes vote. When we fight, we WIN!

COPE Contributions – Our Voice, Our Power.

ATU 587's political endorsements and campaign contributions over decades have gained real and tangible influence and clout. Politicians often seek our Local's preference and opinion on pending decisions and votes that could affect our membership. Here are just some recent examples.

In the last few months, Mayor Wilson's team invited ATU 587 to several meetings on developing a holistic plan to once and for all address 12th and Jackson.

Mayor Wilson sought our input while forming her proposed Seattle Transit Measure. She invited us to her press conference announcing it, and to the signing of the bill.

Seattle City Council members set up meetings with our union to find out what amendments to the Transit Measure that we supported and opposed.

One council member told us that they would pull their amendment if our union opposed it; we asked him to pull it, and he said Done!

County Council member Mosqueda offered to step in when our members at Solid Ground suddenly had their hours cut, as Council Member Mosqueda has never hesitated to do.

We have House and Senate Leaders in Olympia working on potential bills for us on both a Transit workers bill of rights and Washington potentially establishing their own CDL just for inside of Washington to help those losing their non-domicile CDL's. The Governor's office called me for information on this to gauge whether they could help.

Congressman Adam Smith invited our union to a Labor Union Roundtable and offered to hold it at our Union Hall.

The Mayor of Tukwila just added our union to the interview panel hiring their new police Chief.

This isn't about advancing political agendas or political parties. It's about the ability to pick up the phone and get help or action from local, state, and national politicians. Our voice will be heard, and that gives us power.

All this access and power are generated from endorsements and campaign contributions. Those contributions come from the membership's voluntary COPE contributions, automatically deducted from their paychecks.

Please help amplify our voice and expand our power by signing up to contribute to COPE. Even $1 per pay period is a helpful start. For those already contributing, please help spread the word about COPE and the benefits of contributing. Just call our Union office if you need help signing up to contribute to COPE.

Contract Negotiations – Several Members have asked us for more contract updates. While there hasn't been much substantive progress or agreements to report lately, we hear you and will try to share more when we can. With the new SSaM Access Anywhere QR code, you can now report safety and security incidents on mobile devices

(Notice printed with the President's Report on page 24.)

Dear colleagues,

The Safety, Security, and Quality Assurance (SSQA) team is excited to share an alternative way for employees to report security incidents through the Safety and Security Management (SSaM) system without having to use a computer.

The SSaM Access Anywhere QR code allows employees to access SSaM directly on their mobile device (e.g., cellphone) to report an incident, submit a near miss/close call, or share safety suggestions. This functionality was created based on feedback my team received to help improve accessibility and use of SSaM.

SSaM – Security Incident Mobile Update w/ QR Code. Use the QR Code to Report a Security Incident/Drug Use/Other Safety Reports:

  • Using your cell phone, open your Camera app
  • Point your phone's camera at the QR code
  • Follow your phone's prompt to open the link in a browser
  • Click that link and it should open your browser and take you to the SSaM login page

The Maintenance Vice President's Report: Busy Times

By Jeff Stambaugh · Page 4 · Open in the PDF

I have been in office for two years now and it has never been busier. Currently The Union has 117 open grievances, with over 15 headed towards arbitration. Unfortunately, we have spent too much time and too much money defending The Union from Unfair labor practices filed with Public Employees Relations Commission (PERC) over The Duty of Fair Representation and lawsuits. What does this mean? We have a small number of members upset over the way their issues were handled by The Union and have either filed complaint(s) with PERC or have filed a lawsuit against The Union. I am happy to report that in the last two years there has not been a successful case brought against The Union. The unfortunate piece of this is The Union spends its resources defending its actions instead of being able to invest those resources in making things better for all.

We have resolved the Unfair Labor Practice that was filed by The Union over the issuance of Unexcused Absences issued on Holiday work assignments in bus VM. We now have clear established rules around call in times for Holiday work assignments. We are still working on the request for change of hours; Metro is working on updating the request form and the process for management to follow when a request is made. When making a request in the future, please remember, the original intent of a request for change of hours was for a legitimate need (such as childcare, use of public transit) not a want.

I attended the Northwest Conference in Winnipeg in late June along with Paul Neil, Latrelle Gibson, Hassannoor Aden, Tonya Abernathy, Stan Straker, Abdiwahab Adan and Mary Prather. Winnipeg Local 1505 gave a great presentation on their CISM team and the benefits it provides their members. Along with that, there was a great question and answer session with two renowned labor lawyers that covered a variety of topics, including fiduciary responsibility of Union funds. This turned into a much larger discussion with International President Costa, International Vice President Trujillo and International Secretary-Treasurer Kirk. It was very disappointing to hear how some locals were not following the ATU International bylaws and spending money on shall we say, less that appropriate expenditures and attempting to hide it from the membership and the International.

I also learned that the city of Winnipeg has great labor history, including the General strike of 1919 where a streetcar was turned over and lit on fire.

The Union continues to battle with Metro over open, transparent communication.

Metro continues to change and implement new things with absolutely no communication with The Union. Metro claims to want to be our Labor Partner but their actions say different. If you hear of something headed your way, please drop us an email or attend a meeting to make sure The Union is aware, it is easier to work together and implement change than to undue the harm when it is shoved upon us.

The Recording Secretary's Report: Rail/Streetcar Pick and Fall Shop Steward Nominations

By Latrelle Gibson · Page 6 · Open in the PDF

Getting Ready for Pick

All Members are encouraged to utilize the Union Website to preview all the driver timecards, run cuts, and seniority lists. This is also where you can find both LLR Operator and Streetcar Operator absentee forms. If you're experiencing issues with logging on, please give the Union office a call at (206) 448-8588.

Pick Preview Information

Link Rail Operator will be able to preview the work outside of the pick room. For Streetcar, there is no pick preview outside of the 20min each person gets to preview the work prior to their pick time.

Not going to be at Pick?

Please fill out an absentee form. You can locate these forms in the following places: The Union Website, the Rail/Streetcar Dispatch Window(s). You can return your completed absentee pick form to; The window at your work location or emailed to me @recsec@atu587.org. Please remember to leave a good contact number where you can be reached, and if you're going to be out of the country, we can contact you via WhatsApp, just be sure to indicate on the absentee pick form that you will need a call via WhatsApp. If you choose to have a friend pick for you, please make sure they pick no more than 2hrs before or after you pick. For those volunteering to help your classmate, family, or friend pick, please remember that if they pick right ahead or right after you that you will not have your 20min to preview the work, so again, to ensure that you or the person you're picking for will have the 20min pick preview, don't agree to pick for someone that picks within a hour or less of your pick. If I see that you don't have enough time after knowing you're picking for someone else, I will instruct you to go preview the work for yourself, and I can look up the work they want. Remember, this is why the Union is in the pick room, to ensure everyone who is supposed to be picking, can and will pick. Lastly, please remember, Union picks are not subject to the grievance process, so please be sure if you submit an absentee and are not available by phone that you clearly indicate your work choices.

Vacation Pick

This pick is for Jan-March vacation periods. If you're not going to be at pick, please indicate your preferred vacation periods on the absentee form. Please note that the Union does not pick vacations, this is done via the vacation pick room. At the conclusion of picking your work, the vacation pick room will call you to pick your vacation. Please be sure to be available when the vacation room calls.

Not Listed on the Pick List?

If you're not on the pick list, this is likely due to being out on medical or other continuous leave during the shake up(s) and you have not returned to work prior to July 2026. You will want to contact Transit Disability Services and/or your Chief to let them know you're cleared to return to work. For medical, you will want to send your updated medical that clears you to return to work, full duty, no restrictions, to Transit Disability Services prior to your pick date.

Pick Room Phone FOR EMERGENCIES ONLY

Once pick has started, please note that we're in a live pick on the pick room floor and will not always be able to answer the phone, therefore, please only call the pick room during live pick hours for emergencies only, e.g., running late for your pick, not going to make it to pick in time. All other callers' names and phone numbers will be taken for a call back once the live pick ends for the day.

Shop Steward Nominations

The Spring/Summer Shop Steward List is out, and you will be able to find the list in the July edition of the News Review. The list is also available on the Union Bulletin boards at the worksites, and on the Union Website. If you missed the deadline to put in for Shop Steward, no worries, you'll get a chance to nominate for Shop Steward in August. Please remember, every member will have to submit a nomination every shakeup, so for the Fall/Winter 2026 Nomination, all will need to nominate themselves during the open nomination period that has started and will close in August. The reason for this is due to the shakeup being where some may change bases, decide they want a break from being a Shop Steward, want to nominate for Chief Shop Steward, or nominate for Shop Steward for the first time. This is not a live list, so please be sure to read the bulletins and/or the News Review for the Nomination dates. Once the Nomination closes, and the list comes out, please let me know ASAP if you don't see your name on the first SS Nomination list that is posted so I can get you added. Once the second revised SS nomination list comes out, there will be no revisions to this list, and you will have to remember to nominate yourself when nominations open again or send me an email stating you missed the nomination window and I will add you to the list for the 2027 Spring/Summer nomination window. And with that, thank you to all who are willing to step up and sever the membership, you're all very much appreciated by this local and those who you represent.

Hope to see you all at the pick!

40th Annual ATU International Women's Caucus

By Stephanie Binschus, Kitsap Transit Executive Board Representative · Page 8 · Open in the PDF

The ATU IWC 40th Annual Conference was held in Niagara Falls, NY, June 17–21, 2026. Registration was open for 5 hours on Wednesday, and most people checked in that evening. Thursday started with registration and additional check-ins before breakout classes began. During the class time, the committees were instructed to meet and develop a plan for how they were going to continue and create their reports for the business session on Saturday. After my committee met, I was one of the six instructors for the How to Start a Caucus & 10 Ways to Break It Up.

Friday began the typical conference days with Speakers on topics including The Power of the Grievance, ATU Workforce Development, and Let's Talk About Parliamentary Procedures. I would encourage everyone to learn about Parliamentary Procedures by investing in a copy of Robert's Rules of Order, Robert's Rules of Order in Brief, or any of the many other helper books available.

Saturday was the business day and the day that everyone was instructed to wear red for the 40th Anniversary. We had one more speaker, Janet Gehard, the Director of Public and Community for FHE Health, who spoke on The Buddhist Principle of the Two Arrows. Several members were recognized for their role in creating the Women's Caucus 40 years ago, and some special members were recognized for stepping up to help advance the caucus. I would like to express my gratitude to the membership for allowing me to be one of those individuals who have been able to step up and be recognized for assisting the ATU IWC.

Officer elections were held, and the current Officers will be serving another three-year term. The Conference wrapped up with a farewell reception before everyone returned home on Sunday.

Disability Pride: The Work Continues

By Chuck Lare, a Shop Steward & Atlantic Base Safety Committee Chair · Page 21 · Open in the PDF

July is Disability Pride Month. On July 7th, King County raised the Disability Pride flag outside the County Administration Building, marking the 36th anniversary of the Americans with Disabilities Act. Executive Girmay Zahilay, Council Chair Sarah Perry, Councilmember Steffanie Fain, Councilmember Rhonda Lewis, ADA Specialist Cecilia Black, and KCDEN Chair Karla Lynch stood together to declare: "Nothing About Us Without Us." This year's theme: "The World Works Better with Us."

Google it if you want the official statements. I want to tell you what that flag actually means — and why every bus operator reading this should care.

Look at the flag. Five diagonal stripes (colors top to bottom: green, blue, white, yellow and red), each stripe represents an array of disabilities, including:

  • Red for physical disabilities
  • Gold for neurodiversity
  • White for invisible disabilities and disabilities that haven't yet been diagnosed
  • Blue for emotional and psychiatric disabilities, including mental illness, anxiety, and depression
  • Green for sensory disabilities, including deafness, blindness, lack of smell, lack of taste, audio processing disorder, and all other sensory disabilities.

The five diagonal stripes: physical, neurodiverse, invisible, psychiatric, sensory — cutting across a black background. The black honors the victims of ableist violence, the harm done by the lie that "able-bodied" is normal and everyone else falls short. But those stripes don't sit quietly in their lanes. They cut across the black — across the walls, the barriers, the assumptions. That's light breaking through darkness. That's not decoration. That's the whole story of this movement, and it's a story about buses.

In 1983, a group called ADAPT — Americans Disabled for Accessible Public Transit — organized around one infuriating fact: buses had no lifts. Wheelchair users weren't late to work by choice. They were stranded, by design. So ADAPT didn't write polite letters. They rolled their wheelchairs in front of buses. They chained themselves to the wheels. They made the system physically unable to move until it changed.

Then came the Capitol Crawl. On March 12, 1990, about 60 activists arrived at the U.S. Capitol, left their wheelchairs and mobility aids behind, and started climbing — all 83 marble steps, on hands and knees.

Among them: 8-year-old Jennifer Keelan, who has cerebral palsy. Climbing, she said, "I'll take all night if I have to." Michael Winter, president of the National Council on Independent Living, climbed through real pain — not to prove anything about his own body, but to prove something about ours as a society: the barrier was never the person. The barrier was the building. Paulette Patterson dragged herself up on her hands and told reporters those stairs were the invisible wall that had kept disabled people out of work, out of government, out of full citizenship — suddenly, undeniably visible.

Months later, Congress passed the ADA.

It took people crawling up their own Capitol steps, in pain, on camera, to make lawmakers see what had been invisible to them for years.

That's the legacy in this flag. Not a quiet request for sympathy — an active, defiant demand for justice. And here's the part that should hit close to home: the first fight was about buses. Our buses. The lifts we take for granted, the ramps, the announcements, the kneeling function — none of that existed until people put their bodies on the line to force it into existence.

We're the ones who operate that legacy every single day. Every time we lower a ramp without a sigh, wait the extra minute(s), treat an accommodation as normal instead of a hassle — we're either carrying that work forward or we're not. And we need Metro Management to step-up to create reasonable schedules to prevent accommodating to be only the operators responsibility. There's no neutral option. Indifference is a choice too, and it's the wrong one.

The work isn't finished. How will we chose to hold management accountable to support operators providing the accommodations?

Bulletin: Resolution of Holiday Unexcused Absences and ULP

By Jeff Stambaugh, Vice President Maintenance · Pages 18–19 · Open in the PDF

Read from a scanned image on pages 18–19 of the PDF, so it may contain errors.

Bulletin dated July 17, 2026. To: Bus Vehicle Maintenance Members.

The Union and Metro have reached resolution on the issuance of Unexcused Absences for Holiday work assignments. We have agreed to the following language changes for holiday work assignments and overtime assignments.

For Holiday Assignments, please see the new highlighted sections below:

17.3.F. For holiday work assignments, METRO will determine the staffing needs for each shift. When METRO has determined which classifications will be required to work, Employees in those classifications will be offered the holiday assignment in seniority order, first to Employees that are scheduled to work that day as part of their regular work assignment. If after offering the holiday assignment, by seniority, to Employees who are regularly scheduled to work that day and there are more assignments available, it will then be offered to Employees on their RDO until assignments are filled. Should no Employee accept the holiday assignment, Employees from other shifts within the base will be offered the work before assignments are made by inverse seniority to Employees that are scheduled to work that day as part of their regular work assignment. Except in the classification of Transit Parts Specialist (TPS), holiday assignments shall be offered by seniority within the base. With at least 2 hours' notice to Metro, an employee may cancel a holiday assignment.

X. An employee who has volunteered for a holiday shift may cancel without penalty by providing notice to their supervisor at least 72 hours before the scheduled shift start time. If an employee provides notice of cancellation less than 72 hours but two hours before the scheduled shift start time, the absence shall be recorded as excused. If the employee provides less than two hours' notice, the absence shall be recorded as unexcused and will be subject to the attendance procedures defined in this AGREEMENT.

What does all this mean?

If you report your unavailability to work a planned holiday assignment at least 72 hours prior to the start of the assignment no penalty, if you report your unavailability 2 hours to 72 hours prior, your chief may inquire what happened. If you report your unavailability, other than sick with less than two hours' notice, you will receive an unexcused absence and you are able to request the removal through the standard process.

For Overtime assignments please see the highlighted changes below:

17.8.D. With at least 48 hours' notice to an Employee, METRO may cancel preplanned overtime assignment in its entirety. An Employee who has volunteered for a preplanned overtime assignment may cancel without penalty by providing notice to their supervisor at least 48 hours before the scheduled shift start time. When an employee provides fewer than 48 hours notice of their intent to cancel, METRO, if possible, will attempt to find someone to cover the work; otherwise, the employee will be required to work. An Employee requesting and assigned to work on their RDO, who fails to report for work or who reports for work late, will be subject to the attendance procedures defined in this AGREEMENT. For the purpose of Section 17.8.D, preplanned overtime is defined as overtime that is both offered and accepted at least 48 hours before the scheduled shift start time.

What does all this mean?

If you report your unavailability to work a planned overtime assignment 48 hours prior to the start of the assignment no penalty. If you report your unavailability, other than sick with less than 48 hours' notice, management will make a good faith effort to replace you, if they are not able to replace you and you do not work, you will receive an unexcused absence and you are able to request the removal through the standard process

We have also agreed to changes in the request for accommodation of start or quit time language, pick language, vacation language and move up timeline language.

Please see the Union web page for the full MOA.

Signed by Greg Woodfill, President; Cory Rigtrup, Vice President; Jeff Stambaugh, Vice President Maintenance; Paul Neil, Financial Secretary/Treasurer; and Latrelle Gibson, Recording Secretary.

ATU 587 Oath of Obligation · page 4

I, .................................., in the presence of God and the members of this Union, do solemnly promise and pledge (without any reservation or evasion) to support the Constitution and General Laws of this Amalgamated Transit Union. I will keep myself in good standing by paying all dues, fines and assessments required. I will work to promote the best interests of this Union and encourage my fellow workers to become members of the same. I will not take the place of any member of this organization, or any other union worker who may be on strike or locked out. I will not reveal any of the private business to anyone not entitled to know the same.

I will not engage in activity evidencing gross disloyalty nor in conduct otherwise unbecoming a member, and will not knowingly wrong a member or see one wronged if in my power to prevent it. I will not appeal to any legal authority in matters pertaining to this Union until I have exhausted all means of redress provided by its laws. I will be considerate of others in word and action and will never discriminate against a fellow worker on account of race, religion, color, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age, disability, citizenship, or national origin. I also promise to promote the cause of trade union principles and defend freedom of thought, whether expressed by tongue or pen, with all the power at my command.

I understand there is nothing in this obligation that will in any way interfere with my political and religious rights or that will be in any way inconsistent with my duties arising from any of the relations of life.

This obligation I take upon my honor, and solemnly promise to keep the same, as long as I remain a member of this Union.
