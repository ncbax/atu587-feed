Most everyone reading this has had that experience at work - that sudden, jarring, heart stopping incident, that after it has passed, left you shaking and breathless. Maybe it was an E bike that darted in front of you at the last second, or the car that came up fast on your left so it could make an abrupt right turn in front of you into a shopping center. Or maybe it was a passenger who you picked up along the way that started cursing you out. Or worse. Any of these types of incidents has the potential to overwhelm your nervous system which can trigger immediate physical reactions. High stress events can cause a fight or flight response which is your body's automatic, physical reaction to a perceived danger, stressor, or threat. When your brain senses danger it floods your body with hormones, including adrenaline, that prepares you to stay and fight or run to safety.

Once the triggering incident has passed deep breaths, sips of water and body movements are great tools to use to help calm your body down.

Good tools for de-stressing are important for a strong mental health. Critical incidents that you experience at work can build and cause stress or anxiety away from the job. Having positive routines including - exercise, good eating habits, good hydration, fun adventures with friends and family, a spiritual connection - can be positive de-stressors. Miguel Messina, the Program Manager and a counselor with King County's Employee Assistance for Supervisors program, stresses the importance of a wellness practice called Grounding. Physical grounding – standing, walking or lying on natural surfaces allows your body to connect directly to the Earths natural electric charge which provides a calming effect. Mental/emotional grounding - a strategy used to manage overwhelming stress and anxiety by using simple exercises to refocus attention on the present moment. One common method, called the 5-4-3-2-1 method is where you identify 5 things you see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you hear, 2 things you smell and 1 thing you taste.

Great routines and positive de-stressing strategies are important, however, in rare instances these tools are not enough and additional help is needed. The CISM (Critical Incident Stress Management) team at King County Metro can help by providing confidential, peer to peer support to help you process and recover. The CISM support line is available, you can leave a message 24 hours a day and a CISM team member will call you back.
