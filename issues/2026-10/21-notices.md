## Major Religious and Secular Holidays — October 2026 · page 15

**Month-long observances: Heritage & Cultural**

- LGBTQIA+ History Month — honors LGBTQIA+ history, activism, and contributions; October was chosen because several key LGBTQIA+ dates fall within it.
- National Hispanic/Latinx Heritage Month (runs Sept. 15 – Oct. 15) — celebrates the history, culture, and contributions of Hispanic and Latino Americans.
- Italian American Heritage Month — honors the achievements of Italian immigrants and their descendants in the U.S.
- Filipino American History Month — commemorates the arrival of the first Filipinos in North America and their ongoing contributions.
- Polish American Heritage Month — recognizes Polish immigrants' contributions to American history and culture.
- German-American Heritage Month — celebrates German immigrants' influence on American culture, science, and industry.

**Health & Social Awareness**

- National Family History Month — encourages families to research and record their heritage.
- National Work and Family Month — highlights the importance of workplace policies that support family life.
- Breast Cancer Awareness Month — promotes education on breast cancer prevention, detection, and support.
- National Disability Employment Awareness Month — recognizes the contributions of workers with disabilities.
- National Bullying Prevention Month — raises awareness of bullying and promotes prevention strategies.
- Domestic Violence Awareness Month — honors survivors and promotes resources for prevention and support.

**Daily & Multi-Day Observances:**

- 1 — Defender of Ukraine Day — honors all who have fought for the sovereignty of Ukraine.
- 2 — International Day of Nonviolence — a UN observance marking Mahatma Gandhi's birthday, promoting peace and nonviolent conflict resolution.
- 2 — Sukkot Ends (Jewish) — the seven-day harvest festival ends at nightfall.
- 2 (sunset) – 4 (nightfall) — Shemini Atzeret & Simchat Torah (Jewish) — Shemini Atzeret closes the Sukkot season with prayers for rain and sustenance; Simchat Torah marks the completion (and immediate restart) of the annual Torah reading cycle.
- 4 — Feast of St. Francis of Assisi (Christian) — honors the patron saint of animals and ecology; widely marked with a Blessing of the Animals.
- 4 — World Communion Sunday (Christian, first Sunday in October) — an ecumenical observance where many denominations celebrate Holy Communion together.
- 5 — Child Health Day — observed the first Monday in October to promote children's health and wellbeing.
- 8 — International Lesbian Day (LGBTQIA+) — celebrates lesbian visibility, history, and community.
- 10 — World Mental Health Day — promotes mental health awareness and education, and advocates against stigma, with a focus on people with disabilities.
- 11 — National Coming Out Day (LGBTQIA+) — celebrates LGBTQIA+ people's coming-out experiences and journeys.
- 11–20 — Sharad Navratri (Hindu) — nine nights of worship honoring the nine forms of the Goddess Durga, culminating in Vijayadashami/Dussehra.
- 12 — Indigenous Peoples' Day (US) — honors the history and culture of North America's Indigenous peoples; observed the second Monday of October, the same date as the federal Columbus Day holiday.
- 13 — Navy 251st Birthday — marks the founding of the U.S. Navy on October 13, 1775.
- 15 — National Latinx AIDS Awareness Day — promotes HIV testing and prevention in Latinx/Hispanic communities.
- 15 — Spirit Day (LGBTQIA+) — wear purple to support LGBTQIA+ youth and speak out against bullying.
- 19–25 — Waste Reduction Week (Canada) — promotes waste reduction, reuse, and recycling.
- 20 — Dussehra / Vijayadashami (Hindu) — the culmination of Navratri, marking Lord Rama's victory over the demon king Ravana and Goddess Durga's triumph over Mahishasura — the victory of good over evil.
- 21 — International Pronoun Day (LGBTQIA+) — promotes the respectful use and understanding of personal pronouns.
- 22 — International Stuttering Awareness Day — builds public understanding of stuttering and supports those who stutter.
- 25–31 — Asexual Awareness Week / Ace Week (LGBTQIA+) — a week dedicated to educating the public about asexuality and celebrating the asexual community.
- 26 — Intersex Awareness Day (LGBTQIA+) — raises awareness of intersex rights and the challenges intersex people face.
- 31 — Reformation Day (Christian – Protestant/Lutheran) — commemorates Martin Luther's 1517 posting of the Ninety-Five Theses, the traditional start of the Protestant Reformation.
- 31 — Halloween — a festive evening of costumes and community; its roots trace to the eve of the Christian All Saints' Day (observed Nov. 1).

## Articles/Letters to the Editor · page 3

Letters/contributions must include printed names, signatures, work ID numbers, addresses and phone numbers that can be verified during working hours. Members must be in good standing to make submissions. Letters that cannot be validated will not be published. All articles/letters are subject to editing and should be limited to 1000 words or less. Not all letters can be published due to space limitations. Cut off is 5:00 p.m. on the 15th of each month, if the 15th falls on the weekend/holiday, cut off is close of business on the following business day. Any submission from a member of Local 587 to the News Review deemed unprintable by the Recording Secretary shall be forwarded to the Executive Board for final decision to publish. Send letters and articles as original Word documents to Latrelle Gibson at: recsec@atu587.org

## ATU 587 Oath of Obligation · page 4

I, .................................., in the presence of God and the members of this Union, do solemnly promise and pledge (without any reservation or evasion) to support the Constitution and General Laws of this Amalgamated Transit Union. I will keep myself in good standing by paying all dues, fines and assessments required. I will work to promote the best interests of this Union and encourage my fellow workers to become members of the same. I will not take the place of any member of this organization, or any other union worker who may be on strike or locked out. I will not reveal any of the private business to anyone not entitled to know the same.

I will not engage in activity evidencing gross disloyalty nor in conduct otherwise unbecoming a member, and will not knowingly wrong a member or see one wronged if in my power to prevent it. I will not appeal to any legal authority in matters pertaining to this Union until I have exhausted all means of redress provided by its laws. I will be considerate of others in word and action and will never discriminate against a fellow worker on account of race, religion, color, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age, disability, citizenship, or national origin. I also promise to promote the cause of trade union principles and defend freedom of thought, whether expressed by tongue or pen, with all the power at my command.

I understand there is nothing in this obligation that will in any way interfere with my political and religious rights or that will be in any way inconsistent with my duties arising from any of the relations of life.

This obligation I take upon my honor, and solemnly promise to keep the same, as long as I remain a member of this Union.
