Last month I reported on the Holiday ULP settlement. In that settlement we agreed to a language change in Art 17.3.E around request for change of hours. This change gives the Base Management Team (Superintendent/Chief) the ability to allow flexible schedules for family care needs, carpool or use of public transit.

I also reported on how Bus VM management was working on updating the form for a request of change of hours. Management presented The Union with a draft; a few edits were worked out and there is a new form now being used. That is where the good news ends. It seems that VM bus Senior leadership has decided that family care needs, carpool or use of public transit to not qualify as a hardship therefore, they are not willing to accommodate requests for change of hours. Two problems with that! First, the new language *does not require a hardship for a request to be granted*. Second, I thought King County encouraged its employees to use public transportation including carpools. I have seen several denials for a 15-minute change to either participate in carpool or use public transportation. Two of these denials were asking for 15 earlier start and quit time to be able to commute on the Sounder train.

Metros response, "Not a serious hardship, does not qualify" when I spoke to a superintendent about this specific request the response I received, they can wait an hour and ride the next train.

Does management flex their schedules to ride the train?

Does management flex their schedules for family care needs?

While I do understand that management is salary and we are hourly it still seems very hypocritical that management flexes their schedule to fit their needs outside of work to achieve a better work life balance but will not allow its employees the same benefit.

Bus Facilities as well as rail have been relatively quiet this last month.

The Fall picks were held system wide, we are seeing more "missed" picks and frustrated members when they are placed per the CBA. Please do not miss your pick, you can submit an absentee and still call in or show up in person.
