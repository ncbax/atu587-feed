---
title: "Don't Trade Away What We May Never Get Back"
author: "Andy Price, Shop Steward, ATU Local 587"
pages: 12, 13
---
President Greg Woodfill's September President's Report contains a statement about medical-benefits bargaining that I believe deserves close attention from every member.

Greg wrote:

*"And that, for the first time, the Union presented management last week with a proposal for what we believe the membership might accept in trade for agreeing to the slippery slope of a premium share."*

He then explained that if the Union reaches an agreement with King County on a package involving monthly premium sharing, it would most likely require a separate membership vote.

Before going further, there is an important distinction members may not always have reason to think about.

We have two separate agreements involved here. Our King County Metro Collective Bargaining Agreement covers wages and most working conditions. It expired October 31, 2025, and negotiations continue.

Our medical benefits are bargained separately under a separate benefits agreement. Local 587 has historically bargained those benefits off-cycle from the main contract, and our own Union publications once described having a separate health-benefit agreement and the ability to bargain our own benefits as an advantage.

That distinction matters because we are now discussing two major protections at two different bargaining tables: premium-free medical coverage and COLA.

I appreciate the commitment to a membership vote if a premium-share agreement is reached. But there is a question that comes before the vote:

**What exactly has Local 587 already proposed to King County in exchange for agreeing to premium sharing?**

**As of September 15, the membership has not been told.**

That is particularly difficult to understand when transparency has been such an important part of the current contract negotiations. Members have been given access to bargaining proposals and have been permitted to observe CBA negotiations. That is a positive development.

But transparency should apply to medical-benefits bargaining too.

If our Union has already presented management with a proposal for what leadership believes members might accept in exchange for premium sharing, members should know what that proposal is before bargaining goes further. A vote on a completed package is not the same thing as knowing what is being proposed in our name while bargaining is still underway.

Our own history provides important guidance.

In 2013, King County was also attempting to shift more future health-care costs onto employees. Then-President Paul Bachtel wrote:

*"What KC now wants is for its employees to agree to pay for all future cost increases above a negotiated level."*

Local 587 rejected that approach and chose interest arbitration.

Vice President Clint DeVoss put it even more plainly:

*"Basically, King County wants us to start paying more of the cost of health insurance out of our own pockets. ATU said: NO!"*

Local 587 ultimately lost that arbitration. That should be stated honestly. But the Local nevertheless took the dispute through mediation and all the way through an interest-arbitration hearing rather than voluntarily accepting the County's proposed cost shift.

Greg himself calls premium sharing a "slippery slope."

I agree.

Once monthly premium sharing becomes part of our benefits structure, future bargaining starts from a different place. Instead of arguing over whether members should pay premiums, we may find ourselves bargaining over how much members will pay.

Our position should remain simple:

**THE EMPLOYER SHOULD PAY 100% OF THE PREMIUM.**

We should bargain to improve medical benefits and reduce member costs where possible — not create another permanent employee obligation.

The same principle applies to COLA.

At the August membership meeting, while discussing the parties' wage proposals, President Woodfill indicated that the Union was seeking a guaranteed 6% increase in each year of a three-year contract, rather than accepting Metro's COLA proposal, which was capped at 3% per year.

I strongly support demanding 6% per year.

But I do not believe that should mean giving up COLA language.

A fixed wage demand is the amount we are seeking in this bargaining round. COLA is an ongoing contractual protection against inflation.

And bargaining positions can change before a final agreement is reached. If the eventual wage settlement is less than 6% in any year, COLA becomes even more important. Even if we win 6%, COLA should remain in the contract so members continue to have protection if inflation rises beyond the guaranteed increase.

Our own Local 587 history shows that these protections are not mutually exclusive.

In August 2014, Metro proposed a three-year wage freeze. Local 587 proposed a 3% wage increase in the first year plus its existing 95% COLA language for the remaining two years. Former President Kenny McCormick also made a membership motion demanding current contract language and "a COLA with a 2% floor."

The official history of the Amalgamated Transit Union contains an entire section titled "COLA Protections Challenged." It describes "ATU-developed and promoted COLA protection" and recounts management efforts to eliminate or cap COLA during the inflationary 1970s.

The same page shows Local 788 members voting on a 1976 contract providing both a $1.28-per-hour wage increase and an improved cost-of-living formula.

That history is worth remembering.

Previous generations of ATU members did not view a guaranteed wage increase and COLA as opposites. They fought to preserve both.

In September 2014, President Bachtel reported that Local 587 members had made clear they were "*not going to willingly give up their Cost of Living Allowance (COLA) language.*"

The message to both our Union and management should be clear:

**6% *AND* COLA – NOT 6% *OR* COLA.**

Premium-free medical and COLA have something important in common.

They are long-term protections.

They may be extremely difficult to recover once surrendered.

And members deserve meaningful discussion before either one is bargained away — not simply a yes-or-no vote after the bargaining is finished.

Our Union's history should not merely be something we read after negotiations are over. It should help guide us while bargaining is still underway.

**NO MONTHLY PREMIUM SHARE.**

**6% + COLA.**

**DEFEND WHAT WE HAVE. BARGAIN FOR MORE.**

**DO NOT TRADE AWAY PERMANENT PROTECTIONS FOR SHORT-TERM GAINS.**
