If you think back to what originally drew you to apply for this job, maybe things haven’t changed that much? Unless you expected the transit world to be full of glamour and glitz, the core attractions remain mostly intact. I suspect most of us came here for reliable work, good benefits and the steady incomes that go hand in hand with a strong unionized job. This article is about our benefits and the fight to keep them attractive.

We have five different bargaining units in our Local. Four properties are in the public sector, while the other is private. All five offer different variations of benefit packages. Four out of the five properties offer a variation of a Kaizer plan, along with a PPO plan. Three out of the five buy benefits from Washington state’s exchange. The remaining two, contract benefit independently through a competitive bid process. Yes, it’s a bit confusing, so bear with me as I try and explain how this all works.

When our Local negotiates the next contract, our future benefits are part of that. Often, benefits don’t get the attention that wages and working conditions get when members are deciding to accept, or reject an offer, but we should. We are in a time where medical benefits cost are increasing nationwide at levels not seen in 15 years.
