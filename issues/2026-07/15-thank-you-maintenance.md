I can't speak for every operator at King County Metro; that would be presumptuous, but I can speak for myself. And if others feel the same after reading this, then maybe these words will finally give voice to a gratitude many of us have carried quietly for far too long. Because before any bus rolls out of a base, before a single passenger steps aboard, before an operator even clocks in, there is an entire workforce behind the scenes holding this system together with skill, sweat, and pride. Maintenance, Facilities, every crew who cleans, repairs, inspects, welds, replaces, rebuilds, and responds, you are the backbone of this operation, the reason any of us can do our jobs at all.

People see operators every day. They hear our voices, watch us navigate traffic, and sometimes blame us for everything from late trips to the weather. But they rarely see you — the ones who keep these buses breathing. You're out there in the cold, in the rain, in the middle of the night, crawling under coaches, swapping out parts, cleaning up messes no one wants to talk about, and coming to our aid when we break down miles from base. Some folks wish every bus could be spotless every day, but anyone who's ever stepped foot in a base knows the truth: you're already performing miracles with the time and resources you have.

So let me say this plainly, without ceremony or corporate gloss: thank you. Thank you for the work that goes unseen until the moment something goes wrong. Thank you for keeping us safe, for keeping the public safe, and for carrying a burden most people never even realize exists. Without you, we couldn't do our jobs, and the entire system would collapse before sunrise. It is an honor to work alongside you, even if our paths cross only in passing. You deserve recognition, respect, and genuine gratitude every single day, not just when a crisis hits.
