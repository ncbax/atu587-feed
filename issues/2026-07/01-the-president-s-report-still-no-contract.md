Hiring Bonus Update

On 8/14/2023 the Union and King County agreed to a recruitment/retention bonus which was added to the last contract on Page 245.

The MOA stated that these bonuses were for those hired before October 31st, 2025. Recently King County OLR contacted the Union, stating that they had mistakenly continued paying these bonuses past 10/31 to around 50

members and would need to recollect those payments due to not having the budgeting authority to give those bonuses. Additionally, they stated that they would not be paying others who weren’t paid these bonuses.

The Union objected, and King County won’t recollect while they are talking with the Union on this. Not paying a recruitment/ retention bonus that was listed on a job recruitment feels like false advertising, breach of contract, and wage theft all rolled into one. These members should be paid these bonuses.

Ultimately, it’s The County that will decide whether to pay these bonuses or not. Once they make their decision, we’ll take it from there. Do the right thing King County, your word is your bond, keep your promises! True North Values shouldn’t just be a slogan.

Non-domicile CDL Update

As most of you know some of our members you reside and work here legally are losing their

CDL’s. Imagine waking up one day and being told for no fault of your own that the Federal Government has made a rule to revoke your right to your job.

We battle daily with King County Metro Bus and Rail on contract violations, discipline & terminations without just cause. Or not holding managers to the same accountability standards as their front-line workforce.

But we still do find common ground and work together on mutual interests. We won’t hesitate to call things out but should and will give credit where credit is due.

The County has gone above and beyond in dealing with the non-domicile CDL issue by offering temporary employment to those who are affected by this issue. But these aren’t long term solutions, and they don’t make our members whole or wipe away the indignity and injustice.

A Washington State Supreme Court Justice during their COPE endorsement interview with our Union suggested that Washington State could create their own CDL and gave the following similar examples.

Washington State Created L&I because OSHA was not doing enough to protect our citizens in terms of safety standards, wage protections, adequate breaks, etc.

Washington State created WAFSA because FAFSA was not offering aid to non-domicile students and children of nondomicile parents (Dreamers).

Washington State could, in theory, create a division of WDOL that oversees and regulates WA State Intrastate CDL License requirements.

Essentially saying that the Federal Law is not doing enough to provide for the needs of our state residents and we need to write laws that govern our state that are more specific than the Federal Laws.

Our COPE committee has engaged with State House and Senate members who are committed to try and write and pass legislation that could solve this injustice. The Governors office reached out to me and is very interested in this possibility. All of this is happening solely because of the political clout this Union has cultivated over decades of actively endorsing candidates. Please contribute to COPE, your contributions make a real and tangible difference. Our Voice Our Power!

Union Business Travel Report

I spent the first 12 days of June first in Washington, DC, and then in Minneapolis. First up was the ATU International Legislative Conference for ATU Presidents in DC. We spent 3

full days being brought up to speed on the International's legislative agenda in preparation for lobbying visits with our US Senators & House Members that ATU set up. I participated in seven meetings with members of Congress, including one with Senator Patty Murray, who had broken her ankle a week earlier but still attended.

We lobbied hard on issues like not removing or watering down job protections such as 13C and PTASP. We lobbied on AI and Autonomous vehicle protections. I highlighted how non-domicile CDL loss is affecting our members and advocated strongly for transit funding.

From DC I flew directly to Minneapolis for the 30th AFL- CIO Convention. I had the honor of being nominated and elected as an ATU International alternate delegate and served as a delegate on the floor for the last two days. I stood with ATU International President John Costa on the floor as he advocated for a resolution on operator assaults where he recognized Brother Shawn Yim.

There were good updates on current labor battles and priorities, and some good training sessions. I gained knowledge and got good advice from Union Presidents, and International officers, all paid for by the International.
