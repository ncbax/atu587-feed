In my case only the PPO offers coverage for both, and I pay additional money for my choice. By choosing the PPO, it not only comes at a cost to me, but also to everyone in the bargaining unit.

Unless you’re working at SGT, the taxpayers of Washington state are paying the vast majority of your benefits. If you’ve paid attention to what’s been happening with tax monies in the last year, less federal dollars are going to states for medical costs the before the Affordable Care Act was passed into law. Washington state, like most states, are trying to find ways to compensate for the loss, while medical providers are increasing their cost, trying to remain solvent. For folks like us with medical insurance, the cost has increased.

Currently, we are bargaining the benefit packages with King County for years 2027 and 2028. The benefit packages are not purchased through the state’s exchange, but rather independently offered through a provider. This allows for custom modification to plans, and the group rate charges are based on the expenses of our bargaining unit, not the larger group like the state. Simply put, whatever the cost of benefits we use, is what is charged to King County.

Last year for 2025, we had 4,511 members enrolled in benefits for all of Metro, Rail and Streetcar. The total benefits King County paid for 2025, was $93,516,849. If you look at our agreement, or a paycheck advice from last year, you will see the funding rate was $1,694 per month. If you’re good at basic math, take monthly investment, $1,694, multiply it by 12 months, multiply it by 4,511 members, we get $91,699,608. If we take the $91,699,608 and subtract it by the $93,516,849 we used in benefits, we come up deficit of $1,817,241.

For 2026, our funding rate increased by 4% to $1,762. Keep in mind our local bargained for this increase in the summer of 2024. Well before knowing what would happen with the federal funding changes in 2025. It is likely that our 2026 benefit cost will increase more than 4% causing an even bigger deficit.

So here we are today, bargaining for the next couple of years of benefits. Medical costs are up, we either need more funding or to lower our average cost per member to stabilize our benefits. King County’s proposals are for us to pay for the increases, ours is for them to pay the increase. Every fiber in my body revolts against us paying, but we need benefits and have to come to a deal before open enrollment. So, we need to get cheaper and drive the average cost per member down.

In 2018, when I first started attending medical bargaining, our Local was the most expensive group in King County for medical. We had the highest average cost per member and incurred the highest deductible for using our benefits. Fast forward to today, we are the cheapest cost per member and don’t pay any premium share like our bosses. This did not happen by accident, rather a partnership between members getting healthier and a successful bargaining strategy. Folks who do not use medical don’t pay, and incentives members to choose the lower cost medical plan, Kaizer. Year after year, Kaizer is between $6,000 and $3,000 a year cheaper per member, than the PPO. Over the years, we have seen enrollment increase in the Kaizer plan, and a slow reduction in members in the PPO plan. As a result, our medical costs grew slower than all the other groups in King County.

Getting a deal without us paying is one hell of a challenge, the struggle is what makes us strong! This is where I stop, bargaining continues and I won’t put in writing the union’s strategy.

In Solidarity, Cory Rigtrup
