You already know what it feels like when the road is against you — a brutal schedule, a behavioral health crisis at your stop, and backup that shows up late or not at all. What you might not fully feel yet is just how much power you actually hold.

It starts with a story. On May 29th, Metro’s Refugees & Immigrants Support & Empowerment (RISE) affinity group and Equity, Inclusion and Belonging (EIB) team gathered operators for “The Stories That Shape Us.” An operator described her father's Vietnam service and gave us a name that fits: Bus Pilots. A Samoan brother got the whole room clapping with two simple hand motions. A Hawaiian operator couple explained what it truly means to be a chief. Different routes, different backgrounds — one coach.

That is not sentiment. That is strategy.

## Cohesion Is a Safety Tool

When operators know each other's stories, they move differently. They cover each other. They communicate. The bus ahead of you is not just your leader, the bus behind you is not just your follower, we are partners. When you work in unison with your leader and your follower, you stop being commanded by a bad schedule and start controlling your own trip.

That is not a small thing. That is your livelihood.

The more cohesion we build through shared stories and mutual trust, the more authority we earn over our own working conditions — not handed down from management but generated organically from the floor up.

## What We’re Demanding Right Now

Your Base Safety and Security Committee reps are not venting. They have come together to advocate for specific, concrete changes:

• Visible security at highincident stops — site-specific deployment, not vague reassurances. Metro Transit Police need to operate as a full-service model. Coverage gaps need to be mapped and closed.

• Chronic offender accountability — real removal mechanisms and real-time exclusion information shared with you, the operator, not delivered after an incident.

• Real crisis response — expanded CARE teams and KC Outreach Group behavioral health coverage during evening and overnight hours. You should not be alone at 2 a.m. with zero backup.

• Regional coordination — unified data infrastructure and cross-agency partnerships so a crisis on your route does not vanish into a gap between three agencies that do not talk to each other.

These are not policy wishes. These are the distance between what you face every shift and the support that should be there.

The KC Transit Safety Regional Task Force and the Contract Action Team are the vehicles we use to push these demands beyond the individual trip level. Get connected. Show up.

## The Story We Want to End With

There is an operator operating for the longest time who picked off that route. On his new assignment, he received five rider accommodations almost immediately. He will tell you why: his attitude changed when his environment changed.

When operators are supported, riders feel it. Service improves. The job becomes something worth doing again.

The story we are working toward is the day that operator — and every operator running a hard route — can bring that same renewed spirit back with them, because the environment finally earned it.

That is the finish line. Tell your story. Know your leader. Cover your follower. Show up for each other.

The narrative belongs to us.
