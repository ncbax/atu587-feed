**King County Metro drivers keep your buses running on time. The least Metro could do is keep the toilet paper stocked.**

Picture this: it is 6AM Monday at the Burien Transit Center (BTC). You are the bus operator — the same dedicated soul who just safely delivered hundreds of commuters through Seattle traffic — you walk into the end-of-line restroom. What greets you? Let us just say it was not a mint on the pillow – both bathrooms trashed.

You call it in to TCC. You come back on your next trip at 8AM. Still both bathrooms trashed. Tuesday morning, 6AM — both bathrooms are still trashed, but worse — featuring the bonus amenities of zero toilet paper and zero paper towels. You call into the Transit Control Center again — the second day. Response? "Facilities doesn't start until 7AM." A fun fact for two days – both bathrooms trashed with no warning – "7AM start" when the users work 24x7. Metro believe it or not operators need to use the bathroom – entitled to toilet paper. Who else in Metro is required to endure such restrictions?

*"What do I do at the end of the line when I need to take a number two… and there is no toilet paper, Metro?"*

Fun Facts: **48+ hours bathrooms sat trashed; 0 rolls of TP when it mattered; 2014 first L&I complaint filed citing Metro; subsequent L&I complaint(s) filed – here we are over a decade later and Metro still out of compliance… HELLO?**

Unfortunately, BTC bathrooms are not alone. Rainier & Henderson and who knows where else – Metro apparently conveniently unaware.

Now here is the part that really shines — management's bathroom? Spic-and-span with air freshener. Apparently clean facilities are available on Metro property. They are just not available to the people actually driving the buses.

This is not a new story. In 2014, a complaint to the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) cited Metro for exactly this problem. Two "Comfort Station" positions were created, a third position added a decade later. A year ago, another L&I complaint filed prompted Metro to put on its best clean-bathroom performance — and sure enough, inspectors saw spotless facilities. With L&I gone, like clockwork, Metro is back to business as usual — unaccountable/noncompliant. How many L&I complaints will need to be filed before Metro is held accountable/compliant?

Washington state law is not ambiguous here. WAC 296-800-23020 requires employers to provide workers with clean, sanitary, and well-maintained bathrooms. Not "when Facilities start at 7AM" clean. Actually, clean when operators need to use the bathroom.

So: who is going to enforce it? The union? Our Riders might ask, being more concerned about their operator. The Seattle Mayor — the very person who created the Bus Riders Union — might have a thought or two. The answer, dear reader, is yours to decide.

Got an answer or a story of your own? Reach out to your shop steward.

Transforming bathroom complaints into engaging action - Ways to make noise (constructively):

- File a grievance or complaint with WA L&I citing WAC 296-800-23020 — operators have standing.
- Share your documented and photographed conditions every time; timestamps matter for repeat violations.
- Contact the Seattle Mayor's office — they created the Bus Riders Union and care about rider-operator relations.
- Share this story with your riders; the ultimate arbitrator - the public attention moves mountains that internal memos do not.
- Reach out to local media "No TP for Bus Drivers" is a headline that writes itself.
- Require Management to send their bathroom cleaner to support the trashed operator's bathrooms…
