Over the past month, the pace hasn't just picked up, it's been relentless. I've been handling a steady stream of first step, second step, and termination grievances, along with probationary termination reviews, investigations, and proposal writing. At the same time, I've been covering phones in the union office for the vice president and helping members as issues come up throughout the day.

Some days, everything hits at once. I had one day where I was responding to a time sensitive email while the phone wouldn't stop ringing, an operator was sitting in my office, someone was waiting in the lobby, and a staff member was knocking on the door, all at the same time. That's the pace right now. It's also a reminder of how much our members rely on us to be available, responsive, and ready. It's given me a whole new level of respect for our full-time officers who carry this workload every day.

Alongside that, I've been teaching Shop Steward classes, organizing program details, and working on proposals aimed at strengthening how we support and represent our membership. This work matters because it builds the foundation for a stronger, more informed union.

One of the biggest accomplishments this month was working with Arvin Vulliet, Jackie Korte, and Rebecca Frankhauser to finalize the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Committee Dispute Resolution Process. This effort ties directly into the 2021 requirement for labor and management to work together on a Safety Management System, focused on identifying and addressing safety issues before they become bigger problems.

We also reached agreement to bring in EverSafe LLC as an independent third party to help resolve disputes if they arise. That's significant. It's the first time in ATU and an agency have agreed to something like this, and it creates a fair and balanced path forward when disagreements happen.

## Building Solidarity at the PRIDE Caucus Conference

As Founding President and Chair of the ATU International PRIDE Caucus, I had the honor of welcoming members, allies, and guests to Toledo, Ohio for our second annual conference. For four days, we gathered in solidarity, learning, and celebration, building on the momentum of a growing caucus and a stronger union.

I opened the conference by welcoming "sisters, brothers, siblings, friends, and family," and grounding us with a land acknowledgment honoring the Indigenous nations whose homelands we were privileged to gather on. That moment set the tone, one of respect, history, and accountability.

Throughout the conference, we held meaningful discussions on LGBTQIA+ rights in collective bargaining, allyship, and organizing. We heard from union leaders, community voices, and members committed to pushing our movement forward. One powerful message I shared is that an injury to one is an injury to all, and that solidarity must be intersectional if it is to be real.

We also emphasized that coming out is not a single moment, but a lifelong act of courage. Our responsibility as a union is to create workplaces where authenticity and safety can coexist.

By the end of the conference, we had helped grow the caucus to 16 committed locals, including a new one in Canada. And with a successful vote, we're bringing the 2027 ATU International PRIDE Caucus Conference to Seattle.

## Strength and Purpose at the Black Caucus Conference

I also had the opportunity to attend the 59th ATU International Black Caucus Conference, which brought together leaders, members, and community voices for two days of powerful discussion and action.

During the conference, we heard a critical message from ATU International Secretary Treasurer Kenneth Kirk, who spoke about threats to union jobs, collective bargaining protections, and broader economic stability. As a side note he shared nine locals are in trusteeship. His question, with Ice removing immigrants "who will do the work?" cut to the heart of the challenges we're facing as a labor movement. This is a clear dismantling of the middle class!

One of the most impactful speakers was Lawrence Hamm of the People's Organization for Progress, who reminded us that the fight for workers' rights is inseparable from the fight for civil rights. That message was carried throughout the conference: our work is about dignity, fairness, and equality, not just on the job, but in our communities.

The conference also highlighted innovation, including discussions on artificial intelligence and the future of work, as well as leadership development and education opportunities. We heard about accountability within our union, the importance of mindset and resilience, and the value of programs like critical incident stress management, including the important work Cherry Gilbert shared on CISM training and benefits.

It was a space that combined leadership, education, and celebration, reinforcing the importance of unity and forward movement.

## Why Caucuses Matter

Both conferences reinforced something I strongly believe union caucuses are essential to building power.

Caucuses create space for members to organize around shared goals, advocate for issues that matter to them, and hold leadership accountable. They strengthen our union by making it more democratic, more inclusive, and more responsive.

They also play a strategic role, whether it's shaping bargaining priorities, mobilizing members, or building coalitions in our communities. Historically, some of the most significant gains in the labor movement have come from strong, organized caucuses pushing for change from within. At their core, caucuses are about connection, empowerment, and action.

## Moving Forward

This past month has been demanding, but it's also been meaningful. Whether it's handling grievances, building new systems for fairness, or strengthening our union through caucus work, it all comes back to one thing: showing up for our members.

The work is constant, and the pace doesn't slow down, but neither does our commitment. We're building something stronger every day.
