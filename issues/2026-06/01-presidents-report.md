Our Current Metro Bus/Rail contract has 56 Articles, 188,000 words, 542 pages, plus 100 pages of past MOAs to negotiate on whether to extend, incorporate into the contract, or let expire. To start contract negotiations, each side must present its protected position on all 56 articles, either proposing changes or maintaining the current contract language.

Historically, bargaining for a new contract with Metro has averaged 18 months and 60 bargaining sessions. The Union and Metro began negotiations for a new contract last July. As of 5/22/26, we have met for 30 contract bargaining sessions, with 12 more bargaining dates scheduled through August. Progress/movement has been slow but is starting to pick up.

In the spirit of the Union's decision to engage in "Open Bargaining," the Union committed for the first time ever to post our initial protected positions on our Union website for our members to see. It was a lot of work to process and post, but you can find all our union's initial proposals on the Union's website under Contract & Policies > KCM Negotiation Proposals.

Here are some of the things that you'll see that the Union is fighting for:

- A 6% raise for each year of the new 3-year contract.
- Improved break language for Bus, Rail, and Streetcar Operators.
- Longevity Pay for all of our members.
- A 7.5% safety premium for members working graveyard shifts in the field who aren't currently receiving a shift differential. And increases in some shift differentials and premiums for the classifications that currently receive them.
- A market adjustment for the trades in maintenance who didn't receive one in the last contract.

I strongly encouraged the county to allow us to post its protected positions and told them we would inform the members if they refused. The County declined to authorize us to post their proposals. In short, the county's initial proposals include:

Mediocre wage increases, restricting overtime if you work less than 40 hours a week, and disciplinary language that the Union believes would water down just cause.

At the encouragement of an ATU International recruiter, we decided to form a Contract Action Team (CAT). I appointed Tamieko Cook to lead this team. We have brought in a total of 150 members to the Union Hall for a 2 ½-hour training on being a Contract Action Team member. Some AB/CB CAT members held a couple of internal organizing meetings at their base, and some CAT members from SB are scheduling Zoom meetings.

The CAT team's purpose is to help inform, educate, unite, and activate our membership as we transition from our past closed bargaining model to our local's first attempt at open bargaining.

The CAT team's short-term goals are to increase membership knowledge and participation in contract negotiations. Its long-term goals are to build an internal organizing model that increases membership participation in and ownership of the Union.

## Benefits Bargaining Update

Up until around 15 years ago, we bargained for our benefits as part of the coalition of King County Unions. At that time, our Union decided we could do better by withdrawing from the coalition and started negotiating our medical benefits separately. While that strategy initially seemed questionable, it has proven to be beneficial in the long run.

When our Union withdrew from the coalition, a Protected Fund Reserve was set up for ATU 587's Benefits. Per the agreement that established this fund, if the fund falls below $4 million, Metro and the Union are required to negotiate a solution to increase the fund's balance. If an agreement can't be reached, it will go to mediation.

Every two years, we meet with the County to renegotiate our medical benefits. We negotiate the County's monthly contribution per employee, and any other necessary changes to keep the fund above $4 million. This bargaining is separate from Contract bargaining.

This year it happens to line up with our contract bargaining, and we recently held our 2nd benefits bargaining session. The County always comes with its dire projections on anticipated cost increases; this round is no different. The County will spend $102 million on ATU 587 medical benefits in 2026. They project that cost will increase to $111 Million in 2027 and $121 Million in 2028, leaving our protected reserve fund with a negative balance, or shortfall, of $13 million should their projections prove correct.

The County's initial protected position proposal is for ATU 587 members to absorb the entire shortfall by adding a monthly employee premium share, along with other out-of-pocket and deductible increases.

The Union's initial protected-position proposal is for the County to absorb the entire projected reserve fund shortfall by increasing the County's per-employee monthly contribution. Our stated on-the-record goal in these negotiations is not to add a monthly premium share; this has been our goal for every medical benefit negotiation.

Last year, the King County Coalition of Unions agreed to a $75-a-month medical premium share. I believe that leaves ATU 587 as the last King County Union holding the line against a monthly premium.

While technically medical benefits bargaining is separate from contract bargaining, the County has made its position clear that it will consider the total package cost of wages plus benefits during these two separate negotiations.

The Union's intent is to keep the membership updated as much as possible throughout both Contract and Medical Benefits Bargaining. Our Voice, Our Power!
