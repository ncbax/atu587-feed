### ADA Placards:

The County has news, and they have been unable to find money in the budget and will not be moving forward with providing ADA placards on the fleet at this time. Metro cited they don't have $500,000.00 to make stickers. The Union would like to know if we can get a commitment from Metro that all new buses will have that, like the 89 new BEB Gillig buses? That's something that can be requested through the procurement committee. Is it required by ADA laws? No, it's not required, but it's basically information for the customers to show what the requirement is, and the intent was to give support to the operators.

### Building a Successful Probationary Program for FTO:

Metro has got several things in place and new operators being assigned to mentors and follow up with training and follow ups. Metro needs to practice them consistently. The Union notes that if someone has a hearing next week, and Metro reaches out to the Union so we can extend probation, the Union will respond. Regarding probationary points, the Union has heard rumors of them and when you get to the end of the period, the Chief will just decide if you've successfully completed probation. Can the County clarify? Metro claims there are no contractual requirement to use points, so the use of them in the probationary termination letter is going away. Is the system still being used, or is the whole system being thrown out? Will it be a known process, or the whim of whomever wants to decide. Metro is leaving it to the Chief, and it won't be referenced in the termination letter. They will be evaluating at the staff meeting. The Union notes that we have had a process for we don't know how many decades and there were points and if you crossed that threshold, you knew you were done. Are we hearing that that won't be used at all, and each Chief will decide on whatever criteria they decide to use? What Metro is trying to do is get away from that and let the Chiefs do their reviews and being accountable, so they can talk to the employees if they are on a bad track.

The Union can't express how upsetting this is because nobody is going to know how to finish probation.

This doesn't sound like an open and fair process and is a disaster waiting to happen. The Union is going to push back on Joint Leadership pretty hard on this. Metro can do a lot more to help new operators. You must have a standard if you're going to hold people to it and we're not hearing the Chief's are actually reaching out. Are Metro and The Union in this together? We're not hearing from management. We've been through this process at one base and there seems to be miscommunication and the language for direct full time is not in the contract and it makes this discussion imperfect. The points are a tool for metro to readdress someone coming back in 6 months. Metro insists the points just aren't being referenced in the letter. Do we all know how many times we've had probationary firing? Metro contends that less than 5% have probationary firings from performance issues. The Union pushes back on that statistic. Metro tries to explain numbers. The Union understands the numbers and doesn't appreciate Metro discussing it in a condescending manner in this meeting. Metro claims using points is in the rubric in the decision to terminate, and that is up to the Chief, it just won't be referenced in the letter. Metro is still going to use the form used to address performance issues. Metro wants to be clear that they are still doing those check-ins. The Union believes the sky is falling and we want to see what the criteria are in writing. The way it's been stated, it sounds like the standard is optional for the Chief's to use. Are we just going back to management rights and picking and choosing which employees we want? That's the Union's fear. How can Metro control Base to Base and Chief to Chief? Metro claims they are not going that and, they have a sheet. An Operator with 0 points would never go in to see an update, but if they do something wrong, they will still get the check in. Metro is trying to sell the idea that all firings are being reviewed by base staff. Per the manual page 46, medical verification, are we changing this language? Once the operators come to the base, are they being given clear expectations? Is the only time operators see the Chiefs when they've done something wrong? Why can't the Chiefs welcome them to the base and give expectations? Metro believes they have a process where Chiefs are meeting with new operators. The Union knows that hasn't happened at all Bases. The probationary employees are telling the Union that they are not receiving that. Probationary Employees tell the Union, "We never see the Chief until we're being disciplined." The Union wants Metro to walk us through this; will new hires know what the specific standard is to get through the process? Does every single operator have the same standard?

***Do we not see a problem with not having set standards?***

Metro contends that no one is coming down to the last day and then being fired. Is every Chief going to use their own standard? Metro says, "No." Metro is trying to make it more standardized. If the Chief recognizes an issue, they're going to bring the operator in. Metro insists that it's now less than 10% for performance issues. It doesn't matter to the Union if it's just 1 person. Is the Process fair? That seems to not be the case anymore. Metro insists that probationary employees are "At Will" and they want to fire them whenever they want to and not reference probationary points in the letter. Metro claims they can't determine what the standards are, its Chief by Chief. Metro tries to walk it back and say that no termination is unilateral by one Chief or another. Did the Union understand that there are no maximum points and that any number can be used? Metro isn't aware of a probationary point threshold, and the Union is astounded at the lack of understanding on Metro's part. The Union believes that the threshold is 11 points and did we hear Metro correctly that there's no threshold? Applying the part-time standards to full-time was never fair. The Union points out that maybe we'll use some points and sometimes more points, but it doesn't matter now because the probationary standard won't be used and that's a problem. The Union President wants to see the set standards in writing at the next LMRC for probationary employees. Metro will get back to us. Follow up next month

### Accident Report Ssam Training: Base Computer Improvements:

The Union brings this topic up again, because there are ongoing and persistent issues with computers, network, login, and printer issues at all Bases. Union Shop Stewards and Executive Board Officers work with individuals at the Bases to fill out reports, and they still don't understand a lot of software changes. Metro states that all Bases should have a dedicated terminal. The union describes the process that when the computers aren't working, you have to find a Chief available to talk to, then you have to follow up with them to tell them what the issue is and the Operators don't have the time to do all this run around and the equipment needs to be maintained by the employer without needing to use an exorbitant amount of time and it is incredibly inefficient. Operators shouldn't be responsible for maintaining the equipment and the base personnel need to make periodic checks to ensure it works properly. We still don't understand Origami because of all the updates, and we haven't been trained at all. Other bases have consistent Wi-Fi issues. Metro promises to look into the computers and check with training for availability of classes.
