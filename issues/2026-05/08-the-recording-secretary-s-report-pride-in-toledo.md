Ihad the pleasure of attending the ATU International 2nd Annual Pride Caucus Conference April 26 – 29, in Toledo, Ohio.

On the date of arrival, we checked in, gathered our credentials and were invited to a tour of the TARTA (Toledo Area Rapid Transportation Authority) system. We were all bused to the main garage where TARTA’s Local 697 President, Krissy Bofia, worked with management in putting together a welcome social for us. While at the worksite, we toured the yard, maintenance shop, and operator bull pin. After leaving the worksite, we were bused to TARTA’s main transportation hub in the city center. I had the pleasure of being the only one on the bus and was able to have a great conversation with the operator and ask about their working conditions, management support with unruly passengers, and how they deal with unruly passengers. Once we arrived at the transit hub, we were given a tour of their facilities there. This is where the IVP and I grilled a manger on issuing discipline and when the operators are invited to provide input for their workspaces and what this looks like. I did make the comment that I wished we were being guided by an actual boots on the ground transit operators, and not management, but was happy that the Union President was able to help get this organized.

## Conference Day 1

ATU International Pride Caucus President, Michael Mackay opened the conference with a Land Acknowledgment. After the invocation, we learned the history of the labor movement and queer history from the yester queer group. We heard from the honorary speaker, Tammy Parish, who spoke on Women’s history month and her being the first woman in the maintenance role at TARTA.

Mayor Wade Kapszukiewicz spoke about the lowering crime rate in Toledo, private companies not recognizing Unions in the 30’s, Toledo’s history of sit down strikes, Toledo being ahead of the curve when it came to LGBTQIA+ rights, being the first city in Ohio to offer medical benefits to domestic partners of city workers, and about Toledo not making time for the divisions and un-inclusiveness of their population.

IVP Carly Allen welcomed all of us to Toledo and was happy to host and show us what Toledo has to offer. She also thanked the Pride Caucus Board for selecting Toledo to hold the conference, knowing other offers were made by other locals to host the caucus.

We heard from the ATU Local 113 Attorney, Simon Blackstone, who spoke about LGBTQIA+ rights in the workplace, and writing protections in the CBA for these rights.

## Panelist and facilita- tor, Lillian Brents on how to be an ally:

Ways to take action as an ally:

• Sit down and listen to the LGBTQIA+ community • Speak out against injustices, don’t wait until it happens to you or someone you know • Try taking these actions back to your local • Listen and learn • Stop listening to respond • Allies showing up to support and learn, and those allies bringing more potential allies to these events to learn • Work on listening

How to be an ally:

• Acknowledge your privilege • Listen actively • Educate yourself • Speak out on Anti-LGBTQIA+ statements and jokes • Negotiating for your ENTIRE unit

Why be an ally:

• Promote equality and justice • Create a safer and more inclusive environment • Amplifying marginalized voices • Personal growth and learning • Building stronger Unions • Taking actions • Challenging bias and stereotypes • Creating a workplace that values diversity and inclusion

We also heard from Karima Howard who spoke on the struggles on the fight the LG- BTQIA+ community faces. She spoke on her own experience with her granddaughter struggling with her sexuality and her not thinking anything of the behavior change, discounting this as normal teenage issues. Her granddaughter tried to commit suicide by taking pills because of this struggle, and Karima spoke to why it’s important to address these issues early and not put them on the back burner, because you never know what someone is struggling with.

Other panelist spoke on how they stood up for their family members struggling with their sexuality, with the President of Local 697 Krissy Bofia, spoke about her mother losing custody of her and her siblings and the threat of being placed in foster care unless a family member would step up and take them in. The only family member who would step up was her aunt, who is gay, and this was a fight with the state due to her aunt being gay, however, luckily, the courts allowed for her aunt to take them in. Experiencing this firsthand and defending her aunt against homophobic comments made by her teacher has made Krissy an ally since she was a child.
