The Saga of Facilities maintenance continues. While the Pick was finally posted and conducted, the final posting was not what we had hoped to see, while the number of “Usually shelter maintenance positions” and “supplementals” were reduced, it appears that games are still being played to erode seniority and limit picked work out of South Facilities. TFD management now has an opportunity to update the route sheets to reflect the work that is being done and post the work for employees to pick in the fall. I would suggest that The Union and management put a joint committee together to work through this process.

The Union has also Filed an Unfair Labor Practice (ULP) against Metro for grievance retaliation. It has appears that TFD management has retaliated against our members for exercising their protected right to file grievance(s). We will let PERC decide.

Speaking of ULP, we are scheduled to meet with PERC in early May over the ULP filed for Bus VM issuing unexcused absences for Holiday work assignments. More to come.

After the VM bus pick, many of our members requested a change of hours to meet the work life balance we all strive to achieve. Most of these 15-to- 30-minute requests were for

Childcare or Carpool, and VM has been granting many of these types of requests for years. Not anymore apparently. First, I would like to thank the chiefs and superintendents who were able to accommodate their employee’s needs, additionally I would like to thank the superintendents who took the time to sit down and listen to their employees needs and provide reasonable feedback on why they felt they could not meet the accommodation, while we may not agree on the ability to accommodate, we have a better understanding of why you denied the request. All that being said, I am a bit confused at the number of denials that were sent to the Union and the various information included (or not), some simply denied by the superintendent “Does Not Meet Business Needs,” others with with chief approval, and a denial from the superintendent “Does Not Meet Business Needs”. Many of these denials were from CSC, which only added to my confusion, most of our members at CSC have routine work that they perform daily, and they usually know what that work is, and what is needed from them. While what they do every day greatly contributes to the day to day VM operations, no one in management has been able explain to me how a 15–30 minute schedule change “Does Not Meet the Business Needs,” very few employees at CSC receive or provide turnover to the next shift, so I ask of senior leadership, “What business need does this not meet?” I always thought King County was a benevolent employer and could reasonably accommodate these types of requests to help achieve a greater work life balance. Maybe I need to rethink that?

One last thought, FIFA. The Union has been asking leadership for the past few months if OT will be used prior to FIFA to be prepared, in the various responses and through other conversations is appears that management has taken the position that if OT is limited then AC requests will also be limited and more employees will be at work and not requesting time off, I actually had one “manager” tell me “if we limit OT, they wont have AC to take off.”

Way to boost morale!
