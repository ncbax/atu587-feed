On March 21, a spirit of empowerment filled the ATU 587 halls “I’m Every Woman” was sung, setting the tone for an evening dedicated to honoring women across generations and professions. Esther Hankerson, President of the Women’s Caucus, opened the event by welcoming all who identify as women and reaffirming the caucus’s commitment to inclusion, leadership, and solidarity.

Standing for Mayor Katie Wilson, Labor Liaison Evan Woods shared remarks emphasizing the administration’s continued partnership with women in labor and public service.

Voices of Determination and Legacy

Tamieko Cook captivated the audience with her story of growing up inspired by her single mother, a Metro employee and union member who encouraged her daughter to pursue every opportunity.

By Michael Mackay, Transit Operator Rep #1

From military service on the flight line to honoring Judy Young, former ATU secretary, Tamieko underscored how her mother’s union values shaped her career and resilience.

King County Councilmember Wanda Luis, the first woman and first Black woman to represent District 2, shared how generations of women from her grandmother to her colleagues shaped her own journey. She encouraged everyone to recognize that “people may know your name, but they don’t know your worth until you get the chance to show it.”

Operator Addie Ortiz, who joined Metro in 2001, spoke about raising her children while building a business and pursuing her dream of becoming a mechanic. After years in various roles, including equipment service work, she proudly declared her determination to join the apprenticeship program, proving that persistence defines possibility.

Women Who Inspire Change

Erica Evans reflected on her family’s remarkable legacy from her father, Keith Evans (ATU 1765), to her activist grandfather, a two-time Olympic medalist who stood up to the KKK. Erica described winning her election with over 67% of the vote as a tribute to her grandmother, who never had the chance to finish law school.

Council member Alexis Mercedes Rink, representing Seattle’s Position 8 At-Large, urged everyone to remember the women “whose names were forgotten but whose courage opened every door we stand in today.” Dionne Foster, also of the Seattle City Council, joined her and spoke passionately about building equity and representation for all women.

Recognizing Leadership and Change

Awards were presented to several inspiring women. Lisa Nault and Rachael Buchanan, ATU 587 Pride Caucus Chair, were both honored for their dedication and advocacy, Rachael underscoring that “trans women are women,” with Michael Mackay shouting “once and for all”!

Other speakers included Corliss Barnes, Penny L. Scott, who called for more visible diversity across all units, and Eric James, who gave a heartfelt reflection on his mother’s strength. Greg Woodfill, Paul Neil, and Latrelle Gibson shared personal stories of mentorship and gratitude, with Latrelle acknowledging Tamieko as a guiding influence in running for Recording Secretary.

Council members Claudia Balducci and Teresa Mosqueda closed the program with powerful words on why representation matters, reminding everyone that inclusion fuels progress and that every story strengthens the collective voice of women in the workplace.

The evening’s message was clear: when women rise, entire communities move forward.
