On March 27th, we opened the Second Annual ATU International Pride Caucus Conference in Toledo Ohio. ATU Local 697 hosted the conference and UAW Local 12 generously opened their Hall to us for the first day. ATU International Pride Caucus President, Michael Mackay opened with remarks welcoming us and thanking ATU 697 President, Krissy Bofia for hosting us in Toledo. We had a roll call of officers and local Pride Caucuses, and I was happy to see that we went from 2 local Pride Caucuses last year to 7 this year!

Toledo passed legislation to protect LGBTQIA+ rights and was the first city in Ohio to extend domestic partnership rights to city employees. Toledo has about 12 sister cities and Toledo, Ohio was the first City in the United States to have a sister city, and paired with its namesake, Toledo, Spain in 1931.

International Vice President, Carly Allen said, “when we go home, we should bargain for everyone.” Carly felt welcomed into this space even though she is cis/white/ traditional family and noted, “we’re here for a good time, not a long time.” Gary Johnson Sr., International Vice President said, “we are all one. We may be different in many ways, but we all have the same goals. We hope we can turn some of this around in the political climate”

Michael Cornelius, International Vice President thanked UAW Local 12 for allowing us in their space. One of the most important things about our caucuses is our solidarity. We can never ever forget that this is not just a fight of one. We are organized and we are true to each other and when one of us falls, the rest of us pick them up. Michael thanked our allies. He noted that the world doesn’t revolve without the allies who stand with us. Without the allies, we are going nowhere.

We had a Moment of silence for our Transgender Siblings who were murdered this past year

Other International Vice Presidents spoke and noted that “As a country, we are going through a tremendous struggle.

Local Caucus Presidents and Chairs were invited to speak.

Some key takeaways were that not everyone has to like you, but they will learn to respect you if you stay engaged. The sky is not the limit; you make your own limit. Do what you can to make everyone safe in your local. Come together any way you can. You can’t make a change by sitting on your ass. Never be ashamed to use your voice!

After lunch, Simon Blackstone, Ursel Phillips Fellows Hopkinson LLP, Canada spoke on collective bargaining agreements and LGBTQIA+

Collective Bargaining Agreements are fluid events, and he ran through a bunch of different language ideas but didn’t try to dictate to us what we need to have in our contracts. One key idea is to change pronouns in the agreement to they/them instead of he/him or she/her to make it more inclusive for everyone. He urged members to add the words “gender identity” and “gender expression.” Ensure the language protects the privacy of the individual. In the United States we have the Civil Rights act of 1964, and in Canada they have Provincial and Federal Human Rights legislation that defines deliberate outing, deadnaming and misgendering defined explicitly as constituting harassment.

I was honored to present ATU 587’s Article 2 Non-Discrimination proposal and TA. There were some fantastic questions put to me afterwards, and several ATU Locals asked for some tips on writing theirs.

There were ally discussions and I felt like everyone, including myself, learned a lot.

International Black Caucus President, Karima Howard gave the invocation for day two, and then ATU International President, John A Costa spoke.

President Costa recognized the work of International Caucus Presidents and Leaders and told us that we have a lot of work to do and it’s a dangerous time.
