title: "February in Action: Solidarity, Reflection, Representation, and Building for the Future"
author: Hassannoor Aden, Minority Affairs Officer, ATU 587/King County Labor Delegate

---
February has been one of the most active and meaningful months we have had in a long time. As your Minority Affairs Officer, I have seen how workplace issues, community concerns, and broader social realities are all connected. This month was not only about responding to challenges. It was about building structure, strengthening relationships, and preparing for the work ahead.

One of the most important moments this month was the Immigration Listening Session held at Atlantic-Central Base. The turnout was strong. Members came ready to speak honestly about what they and their families are experiencing. There were concerns about immigration enforcement, fear of family separation, and the possibility of racial profiling based on skin color, accent, name, or religion.

Some members shared that they feel pressure every time immigration is discussed in the media. One of the most difficult parts of the listening session was hearing members talk about their children. Several members shared with me, that their worry does not stop when they clock in. Some are concerned about daycare centers and schools becoming targets, especially in communities that have already experienced hate incidents. When you are driving a bus or working in operations, you are responsible for public safety. At the same time, your mind is on your child and whether they are safe.

A few members spoke with me, about making painful decisions out of fear. Some mentioned sending their children out of the country temporarily to stay with relatives because they did not feel secure keeping them here during uncertain times. No parent should have to make that kind of decision. It shows how deeply these issues are affecting families.

These are not abstract political debates. These are parents trying to protect their children. These are workers trying to focus on their duties while carrying emotional stress that most people do not see.

As Minority Affairs, it is important that leadership understands the full picture. When members are worried about their children's safety, it impacts concentration, morale, and overall well being. A safe workplace must also recognize the broader realities employees are facing outside of work.

Others who spoke with me about relatives who are undocumented or in temporary status, and the anxiety that creates at home. A few described how quickly rumors can spread in immigrant communities, increasing fear even when the facts are unclear. These are not small issues. They affect focus, mental health, and overall well being.

King County has one of the largest immigrant populations in the state. Many Metro employees are first generation immigrants. They are hardworking individuals who show up every day to serve the public. They are raising children here while supporting family members abroad. Some send remittances every month. Some are helping siblings or parents navigate paperwork and legal systems that can be confusing and expensive. They carry responsibility on both sides of the ocean.

It mattered that Metro management acknowledged these realities and clearly communicated county policies that protect employees. The reminder that certain immigration related information cannot be shared without proper legal authority gave reassurance. It also mattered that ATU 587 leadership stood firm in defense of our members. When labor and management can sit at the same table and commit to dignity and fairness, it strengthens trust across the system.

February also carries deep meaning as Black History Month. For our Black members, whether African American or Black immigrants from Africa and the Caribbean, this month is both a celebration and a reminder. It is a celebration of resilience, leadership, and contributions that have shaped this country. It is also a reminder that discrimination and profiling have long histories.

Several members reflected on how concerns raised in the listening session connect to that history. The idea of being judged first by appearance, accent, or background is not new. That is why these conversations require honesty and awareness. Black History Month challenges all of us to look at how far we have come and how far we still need to go.

Day to day, these issues show up in simple ways. Members question whether they are being treated differently. Some hesitate to speak up because of their accent. Others feel they must work twice as hard to prove they belong. These quiet pressures build over time. As Minority Affairs, our responsibility is to bring those concerns forward in a constructive way and advocate for fairness and equal treatment.

This month was also significant beyond Metro. I had the opportunity to attend Lobby Day in Olympia and meet with elected representatives from our districts. Speaking directly with lawmakers about the realities facing transit workers and immigrant communities was eye opening. Policies around transportation funding, worker protections, and civil rights are shaped in those rooms.

Many first generation immigrants are not always familiar with how state government works or who represents them. Lobby Day reminded me that engagement is power. When we show up and tell our stories, we influence the conversation. Representation is not automatic. It requires participation and awareness. As Minority Affairs, we will continue encouraging members to learn about their districts, register to vote if eligible, and stay informed.

Another major accomplishment this month was the successful launch of the ATU 587 Minority Affairs Committee. This is a milestone for our local. Up until now, much of the work fell on one office. By creating a committee, we are building long term structure and shared responsibility.

The committee will help delegate key tasks such as outreach to different cultural communities within our membership, organizing educational workshops, coordinating with other committees, and supporting members who raise discrimination or equity concerns. It will also create opportunities for leadership development among members who want to be more involved in union work.

We are setting clear goals for the committee, including strengthening communication between minority members and union leadership, promoting civic education, supporting mentorship, and building partnerships with community organizations when appropriate. This ensures that Minority Affairs is not reactive only when there is a crisis, but proactive in strengthening unity across our membership.

February has shown us that solidarity requires effort. It requires meetings, hard conversations, education, and organization. From the Immigration Listening Session, to Black History Month reflections, to Lobby Day in Olympia, to launching the Minority Affairs Committee, this month has laid important groundwork.

As your Minority Affairs Officer, I remain committed to protecting the dignity, rights, and representation of every member of ATU 587. Our diversity is not a weakness. It is a strength. When we recognize it, organize it, and stand together across race, language, culture, and background, we build a union that is stronger, more informed, and better prepared for whatever comes next.

Thank you to every member who showed up, spoke up, and stepped forward to lead. The work continues, and together we are moving in the right direction.
