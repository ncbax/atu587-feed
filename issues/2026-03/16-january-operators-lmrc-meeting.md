Back Door Entry outside of CBD:

The concern was going to the card readers at the back door, and a bulletin was put out that stated it was the Operator’s call to open the back door for entry or not. It’s a safety concern for Operators and is a critical tool for operators to not have to use the back door. Are we going to all door boarding and is that discretion being taken away from the Operators? What is Metro doing to educate the public? Metro can’t answer that. The bulletin says, “While we are..” Does that mean the policy is going to change? Is Metro trying to implement all door boarding? If we are, we want to know that now. Operators need to feel safe. There’s a reason this was put out and the first sentence pretty much spells it out. If it’s not an issue, then why was that bulletin put out? The recollection is that Metro started putting readers at the back door with the intent of going to all door boarding and it was apparent that it was an issue and that’s why the second bulletin was put out. Customer education is needed at this point. It was a great theory, but Metro has now backtracked. If you put out a bulletin to tell the Operator, that does nothing to educate the public. This will be a long-term process, but we’re going to see operators who don’t care about the rules and are causing issues for operators who are. Operators need support. We can use loaders, Fare enforcement officers, etc... Metro complains that we won’t solve this here and thinks we shouldn’t discuss it.

The Union points out that this is an Operator LMRC and this is an issue that Operators face. The Union would like the Metro Committee members to bring it up and take it further. We are going to bring up Operator Issues in the Operator LMRC. The Manager backtracks and promises to take it up the chain further. The attitude coming across from Management is that it’s not their specific concern, so they don’t want to respond. Metro admits there are some things they can directly affect, but some of the overall policy things that are somewhat beyond their control, will take it further and bring it back. Clarification on policy and support of the Operators. Policy in the end is imperfect because the real world doesn’t work that way. Policy should be a guideline, but it should support Operators where safety is needed. The LMRC is a conduit for ideas and concerns to flow upwards to upper management. It’s a valuable tool so they know what concerns are. Follow up next month.
