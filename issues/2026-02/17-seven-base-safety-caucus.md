Twenty-eight King County Metro operators representing all seven operator base safety committees gathered January 5th for another "Safety Boots-on-the-Ground" safety caucus, addressing critical workplace concerns ranging from protective shields to inadequate break schedules.

## Operator Protective Shields Under Scrutiny

Metro Fleet Engineering's Fariba Fuller and Josh Arreola presented the current Operator Protective Shield (OPS) design, but operators raised significant concerns. The most pressing issue: the shield automatically locks each time operators leave their seats, requiring a cumbersome T-Key to unlock and restricting quick access to the emergency assistance button. Not all operators consistently have their T-Key accessible.

Additional OPS concerns include ensuring adequate space for large wheelchairs per ADA requirements, addressing the open space left when fare boxes are removed, and the inability to comply with policies requiring the shield to block the aisle during ramp deployment. Metro may consider a fully enclosed operator barrier with operator's exclusive front door use and rear wheelchair lift access for future bus purchases. Operators await responses on how these concerns will be addressed.

## Communication System Improvements Needed

Operators reached consensus on requested changes to the DDU's public service announcements and messages. Key concerns include TCC frequently overlooking operators' DDU Send Messages, negating their purpose. Operators requested that pressing the emergency assistance button display "Call 911" on outside signs, and that they receive notification whenever live camera monitoring is activated.

## Base Safety Committee Operations Vary Widely

A survey revealed substantial differences in how base safety committees operate. Monthly hours detailed for committee work range dramatically from just one hour at East Base to 16-18 hours at Atlantic, Central, and South bases. Guest attendees at meetings typically include representatives from SSQA, Speed and Reliability, Vehicle Maintenance, Comfort Stations, Service Quality, Scheduling, and TCC.

Committees identify unsafe locations through word of mouth, tabling, official forms, Transit Safety Advisors, and management reports. However, shared challenges include difficulty tracking issues to resolution, lack of computer access between meetings, miscommunications, delayed responses, and insufficient follow-through.

## Break Time Crisis

Operators identified a critical safety issue: inadequate break time between trips prevents proper mental and physical recovery, creating unsafe conditions for both operators and the public. Current schedules combine break time with bathroom access time, forcing operators to choose between necessary breaks and basic biological needs.

Affected operators my want to collectively file workplace rights complaints with Washington State L&I's Employee Standards Program and, for Seattle routes, with the City of Seattle. These collective filings could provide relief for operators facing these untenable scheduling conditions.

## Additional Safety Priorities

Operators called for enhanced safety training, particularly programs like the Red Kite Project that build resilience for handling difficult on-the-job situations. The caucus emphasized that documenting and publishing safety issues creates accountability and establishes public evidence of known hazards.

Other concerns raised include clarifying Metro's policy on auditing live camera monitoring usage, improving communication methods for unsanitary passenger situations, displaying rolling messages on interior overhead signs, connecting ramp deployment with automated announcements, and automating "last stop all passengers must exit" announcements.

## Moving Forward Together

The caucus concluded with action items including planning the March quarterly "Safety Boots-on-the-Ground" meeting and encouraging collective workplace rights complaints regarding break time issues.

Operators' consensus emphasized the importance of operator engagement: follow up with your base's safety committee members elected to represent your interest in safety, and your elected union representative to the Joint Union and Management Safety Committee. We are better together and when we demand nothing about us without us; whereas we cannot necessarily fix all we face, we certainly cannot fix what we do not face.

The caucus demonstrated that when operators from all seven bases unite on safety concerns, they create a powerful voice for workplace improvements that protect both transit workers and the riding public.
