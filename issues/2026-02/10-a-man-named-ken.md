---
title: "A Man Named Ken"
author: Cory Rigtrup, VM Board Rep
pages: 8
---
Like fire, this guy with slicked back hair walked into my life. Now he is gone like wind over the sheets. Love you Ken Price, keep living till the air leaves your lungs.

I first met Ken over a decade ago. He presented as a cleaned up, blue collar heavy hitting union steward. Quick with the lip, and sly enough to get you to agree with him. At that time, Ken had a reputation for being likable, hardworking, emotionally charged and popular. Destiny collapsed our circles, where I learned the strategic and manic side of Ken, which of course is nature's building blocks for genius.

Ken possesses several priorities that I value, family, love, fighting and money. He also displays a few behaviors that masquerade as unpredictable, erratic or surprise. That is the beauty of Ken Price, loyal as hell, but don't know what he's going to do.

Ken ran his political campaigns the same way I did, by reputation. He would later tell me that he ran for president because he wanted to help people, and why not he had the hair. In 2018 when we started sharing ridiculous hours, I would grow to learn he still supported people who disagree with him and refused to talk bad about them, even in private.

I remember the summer of 2019, killing time with him in Port Townsend. Discussing lessons learned from childhood and how "mom's" teachings should filter into a contract. Acceptance, repentance and redemption. Then strategize about the next few plays on how to make it happen. Years later, those childhood lessons of how people should be treated when mistakes are made, have filtered into many contacts and many jobs have been saved.

I also remember a time when our Local built coffins and marched in Seattle, only to be marched by a Me-2-protest. It was cool, we had a marching band, Ken spoke like a bad-ass, it was so much fun!

Shortly after came a couple hundred layoff, followed by a couple hundred vaccination termination. Ken stated this Local is going to fight, every member gets representation. He knew the vaccinations would bear a heavy polarizing cost, Ken kept silent on his personal views and fought through the ugliness as a president should. That part of Ken I truly admired, when the chips are down, just keep swinging.

I sat in countless meetings with Ken, in every property. He would often reserve speaking and let others pound the position. His method of operation was to listen, reserve judgment, and make decisions later, often after his walks. However, in those meetings, when things got tough, he would revert back to how this affects the operator in the seat. Ken would become emotional, language aggressive, voice sharpen, and he would start overtalking everyone until he had enough, then blast out of there like a dynamo. This mania was Ken's true power, it was awesome! Because inevitably the phone would ring, the answer was always the same, "*I don't know where Ken is at, but he's pissed, so you better change your position.*"

What I will miss most about Ken is our early morning talks. Both of us would come in before the office opened to focus on certain things before the drudgery of work pulled us away. Always positive, we would talk about our families and run through life goals. When the conversations switched to work, we would talk strategy. He would float ideas of what he wanted to do, and I would layout out the pieces that needed to be on the table. It was jovial, like two kids planning to rob a candy store. These scheming conversations went on for years, and when it switched to money or benefits, the electricity was incredible. Ken wanted our Local to be number one, both in wages and benefits. Ken fought above our Local's weight and delivered!

Ken's exit strategy is true to his nature, unpredictable. But then again, is it? 65 years old, house in Neveda, hide your cards and play your game. Love you Ken Price, thank you for all the memories!
