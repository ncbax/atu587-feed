Both personally and in my capacity as President of ATU Local 587 I stand in solidarity with ATU Local 1005 and their membership in Minneapolis as they deal with the effects of the large deployment of ICE and its current tactics in their community.

Just like our Local they have a large segment of members who are legally living and working in this Country but must now live in fear of possibly being stopped, detained, deported solely based on their appearance or accent.

Union members do and should expect their Union to support them when their employment is put in doubt, and their safety on the job is placed at risk. Union officers work for the members and should always represent their interests and wishes to the best of our ability and judgement. Many of our members are asking where the support, preparation, and direction from both their employer and their Union is in case Seattle becomes the next Minneapolis?

The Union has set up a meeting with senior Metro management and the members calling for support to hear their concerns and experiences. And to develop plans and guidance going forward.

Up until now, the Union, Metro, and the County have

By Greg Woodfill shared the same concerns about not taking actions that could draw attention from ICE to our diverse workforce and the potential negative effects on our members. We’re now well past the point of remaining silent without direction or plans.

This is solely about Unionism and collectively standing up for, protecting, and fighting for fellow Union members and NOT about politics, or which side of our nation’s bitter political divide that you stand on or identify with. A harm to one is harm to all!

Our Local, through our members’ voluntary donations to COPE, has a long history of supporting politicians and initiatives that support public transit, our local, and Unions in general. We do NOT use members’ dues for this or support or represent a political party or a party’s agenda.

Our COPE committee sends its recommendations on elections to the E-board and, subsequently, to the membership, based on interviews with politicians. The membership has the final say on these recommendations, and it’s just a recommendation for members to know who the COPE committee believes will be more supportive of Transit and Unions. It’s NOT the Union telling you who to vote for, or which party to support. It’s NOT the Union attempting to represent your political point of view for you.

Please consider donating to or increasing your contribution to COPE. This Union has significant political clout on local transit funding and laws that directly affect our jobs and working conditions, thanks to our long history of endorsements. A portion of your donation goes to the International COPE committee, which lobbies our interests with national and state politicians on transit funding, laws, bills, and initiatives.

You could not like, agree with, or participate in our system of politics/lobbying and still recognize the need for your Union to advocate for and protect your interests within the system we are forced to deal with.

We need to be players in this game to have our voices heard, our power felt, and our interests properly represented. It has historically worked out very well for this Union to do so.

New and future generations of ATU 587 members must learn about and choose to participate in COPE if we want to maintain the political clout that we’ve spent decades building through voluntary member donations to COPE. It’s everyone’s responsibility who understands this to talk with their peers about COPE and its importance. It’s your Union; it requires the membership's active participation to remain viable and strong.

The 3rd VP

You’ll see in this News Review a bylaw proposal to add a third Vice President. The Full Time Officers have spent the last year and a half trying to find the perfect formula for adding an additional VP for this local and there just isn’t a perfect solution for everyone. We are pushing forward with what we believe is the best solution that is a win-win for all members. With now 4,800 active members two Vice Presidents are not sufficient to properly deal with the amount of work thrown at them on a daily basis.

If we had a VP for all of rail including VM it would take too much work away from VP two, go back to a VP representing both operations and VM, and not lighten the load of VP 1 enough.

With a new VP representing Rail Operations and our other four properties it is a perfect balance of total members for VP 3, and lightens the load of VP 1 which allows VP 1 to Just focus on Metro Bus Operations which is a net positive for those members.

The new VP can now focus on Rail Operations and our four other properties that ATU 587 represents. This VP will be voted on and come from these groups. For those who are concerned about a VP needing to have knowledge of Rail, the Peninsula properties and Solid Ground my counter is that is exactly what VP 1 has had to do all of these years, along with handling the thousands of members in King County Metro operations. Should the membership approve this bylaw proposal All non Vehicle Maintenance members of this Union will be represented by a VP with a much lower workload that can devote more timely attention to each members issues.
