It's December and I have returned from union leadership exile. Last month I was sworn in to fill the vacant VM Board position. A position that went vacant when Jerry McKenna stepped down for personal reasons. Jerry is a union powerhouse, so I wish him the best and hope for his swift return to helping out fellow VM members. Enough fluff, here's my article, it's about surveillance and discipline.

There have been some Management changes in VM, some retired, some got fired, some forced to retire. Well, the vacated dominoes of invertibility happened to fall is such a way, that a "game of thrones" scenario formed. No, there are no dragons, but there is the conquest of desirous hopefuls pitting themselves against each other for just a few positions. While I believe in nature's way of competition breeding greatness, this story is not about that.

From the maintenance perspective, Ryerson base hasn't really changed over the years. Same old narrow shop doors, remote parking lot, and the base is weirdly configured to be in the middle of the yard. What has changed is the crew.

Since covid, Ryerson along with all the other bases, radically grew in Utility Service Workers. Utility Service Workers, aka USW, inside wash, even called bus cleaners, have the lowest starting wage of all 88 job classifications in the Metro bargaining agreement, except for Supported Employees.

The USW classification for the last 5 years has been a revolving door of new hires. While many have seen promotions to other positions, most have stayed put and advanced up the wage table. However, a very disproportionate large number, and I would say over half of all of VM' terminations in the last 5 years, were fired.

As the years went on, Metro absolutely failed these members, by not providing them with regular leadership. This issue was the number one issue discussed at VM labor management meetings over the years. So, in 2024, when VM posted a Leaderships recruitment for these crews of USWs, I saw light of redemption.

Fast forward till a few months ago. Some bases have used these additional Leads to train, organize, report and run these USW crews. Other bases didn't get the memo and did something different. Ryerson just failed.

Back to the "Game of thrones" scenario. Ryerson got some new management, and it was not going to be run with leadership for USW crews, but with video surveillance and discipline.

One night, management came in early and did a random check of USW work. They determined some of the work was not up to standard, and not all the employees were where they thought they should be. For whatever reason, Ryerson base never issued their USW crews radios or even provided them with a Lead to report too. When not all the employees were found in the weirdly configured base, investigations began.

They brought the whole crew in, one at a time and asked them what they did that night. Then Ryerson did the one thing everyone should be very, very considered about, they started pulling massive amounts of video. Not just video of the one or two people they had issue with, but every single person from every single coach that gang worked on that night. The video was watched for the entire graveyard shift and matched up with all the stories given. As of the date of writing this article, there have been at least 5 loudermill hearings, 3 terminations and a couple suspensions waiting for a decision.

I could go on and on and on, about the value of good leadership in a crew environment, but let's talk about cameras and our contract. While the contract allows management to basically manage to failure, the surveillance protection language is ancient and should never be touched. What it simply says in cases like these, if it's reported that there is an issue at work, management may use surveillance to verify the issue. Anything else seen, an employee cannot be disciplined for, with one major caveat. If surveillance shows a major infraction, something like stealing, then they can discipline, potentially justify termination.

There are a lot of good, hardworking people in that Ryerson graveyard gang. Their shifts end at 6:45am, even though their workstations are taken away hours earlier for sign out. Regardless of the standing order to hang loose, the "game of thrones" needed their blood. Stay tuned, more to come.

Even though I am dyslexic as hell, it's kind of fun to write these articles. I am proud to be back. Thank you.
