title: "ATU Latino Caucus Brings Solidarity to the Big Easy"

Transit workers from across the Amalgamated Transit Union came to New Orleans in September 2026 for the 35th Annual ATU International Latino Caucus Conference. Over several days at the Loews New Orleans Hotel, caucus members learned from one another, built power, and took on the challenges their families and coworkers face today.

International President John Costa opened the conference with a clear message: "We won't stand by while our members live in fear." He spoke about immigration raids near transit hubs and about members who are afraid to go to work because of where they were born, the color of their skin, or the language they speak at home. He tied that fear to money pressures many members already know too well: rising rent, grocery bills, health care costs, and automation advancing faster than paychecks.

"For our members, immigration fear and economic pressure are not two separate fights," Costa said. "They show up in the same household, sometimes on the same day."

Costa also highlighted the caucus's history of organizing and honored Executive Vice President Yvette Trujillo, the first woman and first Latina elected to that position. "Our answer to fear is organizing," he said. "Our answer to fear is each other."

Attendees heard from New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno, the city's first Latina mayor, and ATU International staff led trainings on grievances and arbitrations that gave members practical skills to bring home to their locals.

*Photo caption: From Left to Right, Luis Alzate, President, ATU Latino Caucus; Michael Mackay, President, ATU PRIDE Caucus; and Karima Howard, President, ATU Black Caucus*

Latino Caucus President Luis Alzate provided a strong panel on non-domiciled CDLs and what the new restrictions mean for immigrant transit workers. He told me the idea for the session came from the ATU PRIDE Caucus conference earlier this year.

President Costa asked me to speak about Seattle's transit levy, which is on the ballot. I thanked ATU Local 587 President Greg Woodfill for securing the $75,000 our local contributed to the campaign. President Costa and the International Executive Board then doubled that amount. If voters pass the levy, it could add 100,000 service hours to Metro and create many more union jobs.

I thanked President Woodfill for making it possible for me to attend, along with the many people who have supported the ATU PRIDE Caucus. I also walked the room through what we've built together over the past four years.

For me, some of the most valuable moments came in conversations between sessions. I spoke with Raymond Jackson, President and Business Agent of ATU Local 689 in Washington, D.C., and the first-ever recipient of the Yvette Trujillo Award. Local 689 represents 16,888 members under 15 contracts. Raymond told me the local has spent millions of dollars fighting for its members' right to strike, and that it was a fight worth having.

I had a great conversation with Jim Bradley, Financial Secretary-Treasurer of the Boston Carmen's Union, ATU Local 589. He told me Boston wants us to beat $48.55 an hour, because if we go above it, they can use our contract as a comparable in their next round of bargaining. That's solidarity across locals: every raise we win helps lift transit workers across the country.

I also valued building a relationship with Karima Howard, President of the ATU International Black Caucus. She is a powerful voice for our members and a mentor to me.

The city carried its own union history. Members visited Parkway Bakery and Tavern, a New Orleans po'boy institution. The po'boy dates back to the 1929 streetcar strike, when local restaurants fed striking transit workers, members of the union that became today's ATU, and called them "poor boys."

The conference closed with a message for every Latino and Latina transit worker: you are not alone, and the ATU stands with you. ¡Sí se puede!
