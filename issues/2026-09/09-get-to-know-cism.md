Critical Injury Stress Management, or CISM, was created in the early 1970's to help firefighters and paramedics process traumatic stress after difficult calls. To this day, many high-stress professional services including law enforcement, firefighters, healthcare, the military, disaster relief workers, and public transportation workers use a CISM team to help their employees cope with traumatic situations. At King County Metro, the Critical Incident Stress Management team members come from throughout the entire system – rail, streetcar, vehicle maintenance, facilities, and bus operations – offering support that is peer-to-peer and confidential. As employees of King County, our work provides countless opportunities to see and experience difficult situations, and most of the time, the coping strategies we have developed are strong enough and positive enough that we can manage through the difficult stuff.

A particular situation I remember from when I was a new operator occurred while operating my coach along 4th Ave S. and South Lander Street. I watched a young family crossing the street in the hot afternoon sun; they were pushing strollers full of babies and pulling carts packed with possessions. They appeared homeless and seemed to have no real destination. I remember feeling very sad for that family, no judgment on how they got there, just a deep sadness that that is where they were. As I worked the rest of the day, I just could not shake that image. I was raising my own young kids at the time and watching family out in the hot sun, made me feel anxious about the responsibilities of raising a family. At the end of the day, that image and my anxious feelings stuck with me, and I took them home. I had a close family member who had been a Metro operator for over three decades and I spoke with him about it. I also used some of my own positive personal coping mechanisms to help me work through my feelings. I was able to process and move on.

Most of us who work at Metro have had experiences that affect us. Also, most of us have developed strong coping strategies that allow us to let many stressful things go. In rare instances, you may carry images, experiences, situations, or feelings from your work environment that are hard to shake. When your usual coping skills are overwhelmed, reaching out to CISM can be beneficial. All CISM members are trained to provide confidential support to assist you in processing and recovery. Call the Metro CISM Team message line anytime at 206 718-9734 to receive confidential, free, peer to peer services for crisis and critical incident support.
