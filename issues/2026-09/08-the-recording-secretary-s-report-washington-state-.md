## Panel on Workers Decide: The Future of Work

Katie Durkin, WSFE, spoke on the future of AI. She spoke that this is not new for us, workers have faced having to deal with advances in technology in the past. The difference is, we weren’t excluded. When we exclude workers from AI talks, this is where the issue on labor begins. There’s a lot of impacts of AI on the workers and the public and having the right to bargain over this is important. The only way we can accomplish this is by making sure the workers are in the room where AI talks are on going that have a direct impact on workers. Employees of the State of Washington don’t get to have a say in their workplace.

Matt Lim, IBT 117, spoke on AI being more of a digital supervisor. This technology is used in their work trucks. So, the Union wrote language around this to only work when needed, like accidents or hard braking. They also implemented in their CBA that when new versions of AI is onboarded, the language will reflect what it can or can’t do. Spoke to future proofing the language to always be ahead of the implantation of AI. Spoke to really engaging and educating the members.

Jessica Hauffe, WSNA, spoke on the impacts of AI in the nursing industry. Nurses should be involved in every aspect of the

By Latrelle Gibson creation of AI. This would ensure that nurses are involved in the process to be sure the product is working correctly, this would also help make sure the correct products are being purchased. She spoke to AI not being able to do what boots on the ground nurses do. Spoke on HB 2155 that was signed into law June 11, 2026, that updates Washington Nurse’s Practice Act to make clear that only licensed human nurses can use the nurse title(s). This bill prevents AI from presenting themselves as nurses.

## Workshops: Lobbying for Non-lobbyists

Jay C. Joy, OR AFL-CIO, spoke on what is lobbying. He spoke about lobby day and the importance of all workers showing up at these sessions. Spoke about a fiscal note, the amount per bi annum. It’s an estimate of what the cost should be to purchase certain items required to remain operational. Who is required to register as a lobbyist? Those who’re part of the AFL- CIO or other similar organizations. How to lobby? Engage with your local government, keep an eye on what your state council is doing. This information is accessible by a simple website search. The best part of lobbying within your local government is that your voices are heard by your elected officials. You can lobby for your Union here. Most times elected officials do care about what Unions have to say about certain bills, so our voices in these spaces prove to be paramount to speaking up for our working rights and things that directly impact our jobs.

Providing public comment:

Set time (1 minute, 90 seconds, 2 minutes, etc.). Often this is in person or virtual, provide your name and the name of the organization you’re affiliated with, and tell the story of why you care. Law makers tend to care a lot more about those who show up for public comments to speak about issues that can have a direct impact on their work.

Lobbying State Government:

The legislative session is around the corner. The legislative session includes coming to lobby day, which will allow you at 15min max to talk about those issues that impact their community. Planning is on its way for policy work to prep for the upcoming legislative session.

The Lobbying for Non- Lobbyists workshop was also geared towards Lobbying for bills that we would want support on. We were instructed to write a short 30 second pitch on a bill that we would want to push to our Senator while in the elevator with them. I volunteered to be one who stood up in front of those in attendance to pitch my ask to my elected representative. My pitch was to convince my representative, Clare Wilson, to support the bill opposing changes to Washington State’s PFML.

## Workshop: WSLC PFML Under threat

Since WSLC won expansion of the PFML, this win has been expanded to include all families, chosen family, etc., not just one’s nuclear family. There are more ways to improve the program by expanding to those who’re domestic abuse victims.

At the workshop, stories from those in attendance were shared on the positive impact of using PFML, A lot of politicians, even some Republicans, have been in support of the program. I was able to share with the group the positive impacts of those who are injured off the job and need to utilize the program and how this was a great help to many of our members who have had to use the program, and why it’s extremely important that we continue to fight legislation to negatively alter it. This is why I will be at the Capital during the next legislative session to help fight against the proposed cuts to the program.

There have been several bills presented by Republicans to severely alter PFML. For one, public employees wouldn’t have the ability to supplement their income using short term disability or long-term disability. On the plus side, the vast majority supports taxing higher wages rather than cutting benefits.

Non-Lobbyists workshops. At Know your Rights workshop, this workshop was geared towards providing an overview of fundamental rights in the workplace and the community. Participants will learn what rights apply to all workers, how to assert those rights, and what to do if approached by law enforcement or government agencies, including immigration enforcement. This workshop will also cover recent immigration policy updates and current enforcement practices that may impact your workforce.

## Researching AI in the Workplace:

At this workshop, I participated in a meet and greet, where we talked about new technologies at the workplace. I talked about the Zonar system that hasn’t been bargained for yet, but operators were being trained on the system. We also talked about Disney using a computer-generated orchestra in place of real trained people who normally would play in the orchestra. There were also stories of AI being used for grading in the medical field, stories of news anchors controlling their own teleprompter, AI being used that creates biases, such as driverless vehicles being tested in affluent white communities instead of other communities.

I truly enjoy attending the WSLC, I love the ability to network with Labor from all over the State. I also find the curriculum at the convention to be truly engaging, informative, and something I’m able to use to help all members. There’s always something for us to fight against, and I love being one of those willing to take our fight to our law makers!
